## Q1. For each motion, which AI or robotics startups used it to land their first customers, and what did the first deal look like?

### Takeaway
Robotics companies with verifiable first-customer stories almost all won them through some mix of three things: founder-led direct selling into a narrow vertical, a scoped, often milestone-based pilot or POC with one anchor customer, and, once there were tens of units, channels such as OEMs and integrators or RaaS pricing. For AI software, the best-documented recent pattern is the paid design partnership. Sierra charged design partners and converted all of them. Open source and PLG are real for AI software and dev tools. For robotics they mainly work at the developer and hobbyist hardware tier (Hugging Face/Pollen), not for industrial first customers.

### Cited Findings

**Motion 1: Founder-led sales and network mining**
- [OPINION] For roughly the first 20 customers, the founder must own sales personally, and this cannot be outsourced to a salesperson or GTM hire. — [Race Capital, "10 Lessons Learned from Founder-Led Sales"](https://medium.com/racecapital/10-lessons-learned-from-founder-led-sales-for-startups-3b558fd06782)
- [OPINION] "The founder is the sales motion until customer 10 to 15." — [Boomerang glossary, a vendor source](https://getboomerang.ai/glossaries/founder-led-sales-for-first-customers)
- [OPINION, vendor, unsourced numbers] One 2026 AI-GTM blog suggests founder outreach to 50 to 100 candidates and expects 10 to 15% to convert into design partners. — [semnexus](https://semnexus.com/startup-gtm-for-ai-first-products-in-2026/)
- [FACT, robotics example] Gecko Robotics (wall-climbing boiler inspection robots) got its first customer, Scrubgrass Generating Company, a coal plant in western Pennsylvania, through a college senior-design project the plant sponsored. The founders went back with a prototype under an order to inspect the boiler and check the data with the plant engineer. A $10K Ben Franklin seed grant paid for the prototype. Gecko joined YC W16 and reached product-market fit in 2017 with a "handful of Power Generation customers." — [Ben Franklin Technology Partners](https://cnp.benfranklin.org/?p=4043); [Keystone Edge 2015 profile](https://keystoneedge.com/2015/08/03/founder-profile-orion-correa-of-gecko-robotics-llc/); [Fortune 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/04/17/gecko-founding-jake-loosararian-robots-infrastructure-environment-y-combinator); [Gecko About page](https://www.geckorobotics.com/about-us). Sources conflict on the date of the first plant visit: Fortune says 2010, Gecko says 2012. This is an older, pre-2020 example.
- [FACT] Gecko's early deals were inspection services delivered by its own robots and crew, priced per inspection rather than as robot sales. This is a "service-first" wedge. — [TechCrunch on Gecko power plant inspections](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1295411). The service model is described in the article. I did not see specific deal sizes.

- [FACT/OPINION] First Round Review's Sierra case study (Sierra is an AI customer-service agent company) reports that Sierra required design partners to pay, commit time and co-build. It screened out companies that only wanted to "play with AI" or build internal prototypes, and it converted 100% of design partners into customers. — [First Round Review, Sierra design partnerships (search snippet; full page blocked)](https://review.firstround.com/sierra-design-partnership.md)
- [OPINION, secondary and unverified] A roundup of First Round advice says Sierra had design partners pay 10 to 20% of total contract value, with 2 to 6 month time limits. I could not check this against the original article. — [Roundup of First Round's 2025 advice](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/review.firstround.com/the-30-best-pieces-of-company-building-advice-we-heard-in-2025)
- [OPINION] Fero Labs (industrial AI) won industrial giants without free pilots. Its advisor's rule was "don't do free work," even at the cost of losing the customer. — [Frontlines.io on Fero Labs](https://www.frontlines.io/?p=5723)
- [OPINION, vendor stats, unverified] Pilot fees of even $5K to $10K are said to create much more customer engagement. ### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] **Founder-led sales.** Step by step: mine your network and alumni plus industry-sponsored projects for 30 to 100 target accounts, run discovery calls, convert a few into paid design partners or pilots, then turn those into referenceable deployments. Fits all three categories, and is close to mandatory for robotics because buyers need on-site trust. Main failure mode: founders over-customize for one champion with no budget authority.
- [INFERENCE] **Design partners and paid pilots.** Step by step: a written success-criteria document, a fixed timeline (60 to 120 days for software, 3 to 12 months for robotics), a fee (often credited toward the contract), and a pre-agreed conversion price. Failure mode: "pilot purgatory," which is especially common in robotics. Figure/BMW started with a single evaluation robot, and Dexterity/FedEx took "several years" to reach scale-up.
- [INFERENCE] **Big-logo humanoid and general-purpose robotics deals** (Figure/BMW, Agility/GXO) are best read as milestone-gated technical evaluations with RaaS or JDA structures, not volume purchases. Their main value at seed and Series A is fundraising signal and data collection.
- [INFERENCE] **Captive or adjacent customer zero.** Locus started inside Quiet Logistics, and Gecko started from a sponsored project at Scrubgrass. When the founders or parent company operate the facility, the first-customer problem largely goes away.
- [INFERENCE] **Channels and OEMs** are rarely how a robotics company lands customer #1. They are how it gets from about 10 to about 100+ customers. The exception is an autonomy-software company (Brain Corp) that rides an OEM's installed base, where the OEM effectively is the first customer.
- [INFERENCE] **Open source and PLG** work for AI models and tools (Hugging Face) and for low-cost developer hardware (Reachy Mini, low-cost LeRobot arms). They do not produce first industrial customers for $100K+ robotic cells. Hugging Face monetizes through hub, enterprise and hardware revenue, not through industrial deployments.

## Q2. Which motions work best for hardware-heavy vs software-heavy offerings?

### Takeaway
For hardware-heavy robotics, the motions with evidence are founder-led direct sales into one vertical, scoped and milestone-gated paid pilots with anchor customers, RaaS or outcome pricing to get past capex approval, non-dilutive grants and government contracts, and OEMs or integrators once units scale. For software-heavy AI, the motions with evidence are paid design partners, PLG and open source, and cloud-marketplace procurement. Combined AI+robotics companies tend to look like hardware companies in their first 10 deals.

### Cited Findings
- [OPINION] Robotics investors recommend that the startup deploy the first units itself and add integrators at tens-to-hundreds of units. — [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/robotics_invest_panelists_discuss_startup_strategy_vc_criteria_in_boston/autonomy); [F-Prime](https://fprimecapital.com/?p=10592)
- [OPINION] Integrators are the procurement and maintenance gatekeepers for manufacturing and logistics buyers. — [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/five-dos-and-donts-on-building-your-robotics-startup)
- [FACT] RaaS removes the buyer's capex hurdle. Formic charges nothing until the system is operational, then bills by the hour, which let small first-time automators like Compact Industries buy. — [ManufacturingTomorrow](https://www.manufacturingtomorrow.com/news/2022/12/07/formic-launches-first-ever-hot-swap-no-risk-robot-solution-to-drive-manufacturing-automation/19789/); [Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/?p=54965)
- [OPINION] A successful RaaS business is "not just a robot sold on subscription, nor is it simply a rental financing model." It needs service, uptime and fleet operations. — [The Robot Report, "Why RaaS needs more than a subscription model"](https://www.therobotreport.com/why-raas-needs-more-than-a-subscription-model/)
- [OPINION] Per-task or outcome leases "can prove riskier for robotics companies, especially startups." — [Built In](https://builtin.com/robotics/robotics-as-a-service-raas)
- [FACT] Software-only AI can be bought through cloud marketplaces with AWS as seller of record, which shortens procurement. Hardware cannot use this route in the same way. — [Marketeering](https://www.marketeering.ai/?p=7872)
- [FACT] An autonomy-software company (Brain Corp) scaled through a hardware OEM (Tennant) rather than building and selling machines itself. — [GlobeNewswire](https://globenewswire.com/news-release/2018/04/05/1465733/0/en/Brain-Corp-and-Tennant-Company-Team-Up-to-Introduce-Autonomous-Cleaning-Machines.html)
- [OPINION] Eclipse's Seth Winterroth says robotics hardware and software have become "significantly better — and cheaper," and the startup market has matured. — [TechCrunch, Sept 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/12/we-are-entering-a-golden-age-of-robotics-startups-and-not-just-because-of-ai)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] **Fit matrix** (H = hardware robotics, S = AI software, C = combined):
  - Founder-led sales: H strong, S strong, C strong.
  - Paid design partner or pilot: S strong (60 to 120 days); H/C strong but slow, with a site survey, safety review, integration and a 3 to 12+ month pilot.
  - PLG, freemium, open source: S strong; H weak except developer and education hardware (Reachy Mini, about $300); C works only as a top-of-funnel developer motion.
  - Community and developer-led: S strong; H/C moderate, for the developer-platform layer (LeRobot, ROS tooling, NVIDIA Isaac ecosystem).
  - Content and thought leadership: S moderate-to-strong; H/C supportive only. Videos of robots working help fundraising and inbound but rarely close a capex deal alone.
  - Channel, SI, VAR, OEM: H/C critical from about 10 units on; S useful later (consultancies, GSIs).
  - Cloud marketplaces: S strong for mid-market and enterprise procurement; H not applicable; C only for the software or fleet layer.
  - RaaS and outcome pricing: H/C strong for buyer adoption, but a heavy balance-sheet burden on the startup.
  - Government and SBIR: strong for H/C (defense, inspection, space, ag) and for dual-use S.
  - Venture client and corporate programs: H/C strong (BMW Garage buys a "first unit"); S moderate.
  - Trade shows: H/C strong (ProMat, MODEX, Automate, vertical shows like Labelexpo); S weaker.
  - Services first: H/C strong (sell inspections or picks, not robots); S common ("AI consulting to product") but risks becoming an agency.
- [INFERENCE] Motions that mostly do not work for first robotics customers: self-serve PLG for industrial cells, paid ads, generic cold-email volume plays, and cloud marketplaces for physical goods. Hackathons produce developer goodwill, not industrial buyers.

### Gaps
- No quantitative comparison (for example, conversion rates by motion) for hardware vs software was found.

## Q3. What are typical timelines and first ACV ranges?

### Takeaway
Hard public numbers are scarce. Robotics anchor deals rarely disclose value. Sourced data points are below. AI-software paid pilots appear to start around $5K to $10K+ (vendor sources), with Sierra-style design partners paying a share of total contract value. Robotics paths from first pilot to scaled deployment run from about 6 months (Agility: POC late 2023, RaaS contract mid-2024) to several years (Dexterity/FedEx). RaaS pricing for simple cells starts around $8/hour (Formic).

### Cited Findings
- [FACT] Agility/GXO: about 6 to 9 months from proof of concept (late 2023) to a multi-year RaaS agreement (June 2024). — [GXO](https://investors.gxo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gxo-signs-industry-first-multi-year-agreement-agility-robotics)
- [FACT] Figure: founded 2022, first commercial agreement January 2024 (about 18 to 24 months), starting with one evaluation robot. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/bmw-testing-figure-01-humanoid-spartanburg-automotive-plant/); [PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/figure-announces-commercial-agreement-with-bmw-manufacturing-to-bring-general-purpose-robots-into-automotive-production-302036263.html)
- [FACT] Dexterity/FedEx: announced September 2023, scaling announced after validation "over several years." — [FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fedex-moves-closer-to-deploying-robots-that-can-load-trailers)
- [FACT] Shield AI: a $1M, nine-month prototype award is reported as its early DIUx contract, and a $7.2M SBIR Phase II came about 4 years later. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_AI); [Shield AI](https://shield.ai/?p=2371)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] Formic arithmetic: $8/hour × 8 hours × about 22 days ≈ $1.4K/month on one shift, or about $2.8K/month on two shifts. That is about $17K to $34K per cell per year, an order-of-magnitude first ACV for SMB manufacturing RaaS.
- [INFERENCE] **Indicative ranges for planning.** Treat these as rough synthesis, not sourced benchmarks.
  - AI software, first paid pilot: about $5K to $50K, 1 to 3 months to first revenue after first contact.
  - AI software, first annual contract: about $20K to $150K for mid-market and enterprise.
  - Robotics paid pilot or POC: about $25K to $250K+ (often hardware cost-recovery plus integration), 3 to 12 months.
  - Robotics first production deal (multi-unit or RaaS fleet): $100K to $1M+, often 9 to 24+ months from first meeting.
  - Defense SBIR Phase I/II: award sizes are set by DoD rules. I did not verify these in this session (see Gaps).

## Q4. Where do expert sources disagree?

### Takeaway
The clearest disagreements are on paid vs free pilots, where most operators favor paid but some guides allow free-credit trials; RaaS vs capex sale, where RaaS helps buyers adopt but critics stress the startup's balance-sheet strain and operational demands; and direct vs channel for robotics, where the consensus is "direct first, channel at scale" but industry analysts argue channel is becoming table stakes. I could not source the vertical-vs-horizontal debate or the PLG-for-hardware debate from the named VC blogs in this session.

### Cited Findings
**Paid vs free pilots**
- [OPINION] Never do free work, even at the risk of losing the customer. — [Fero Labs via Frontlines](https://www.frontlines.io/?p=5723)
- [OPINION] Sierra required paying design partners and reports 100% conversion. — [First Round Review (snippet)](https://review.firstround.com/sierra-design-partnership.md)
- [OPINION] A free-trial period with usage-limited free credits before conversion can work for startups. Advice is not uniform. Paid pilot length also varies, from 7 to 14 days in one source to 60 days in another. — [CallSphere](https://callsphere.ai/blog/vw7c-free-pilot-vs-paid-poc-ai-2026); [Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/deal-closing/pilot-programs)
- [OPINION] "Free trials are not pilots." — [Medium, "Chart a course out of pilot hell"](https://medium.com/the-vanguard/startup-ceo-chart-a-course-out-of-pilot-hell-9b2b680e73d)

**RaaS vs capex sale**
- [OPINION, pro] RaaS turns large capex into opex and lowers adoption barriers. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/robotics/robotics-as-a-service-raas); [RobotLAB (vendor)](https://www.robotlab.com/blog/raas-vs-capex-why-operations-leaders-are-making-the-financial-pivot-robotlab/)
- [OPINION, con] RaaS is more demanding than it looks: service, uptime and fleet ops, not just financing. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/why-raas-needs-more-than-a-subscription-model/)
- [OPINION, con, vendor] Vendor balance sheets are finite, so RaaS and vendor financing "don't scale." Robotics burn is said to be 3 to 5x SaaS burn at equal revenue, while investors underwrite it like SaaS. — [peaq](https://www.peaq.xyz/blog/why-robots-dont-deploy-at-scale-unusual-assets-need-unusual-financing)
- [OPINION] RaaS margins at scale should look more like SaaS than hardware, but the capital intensity is unfamiliar to software investors. — [Formant, RaaS fundraising](https://formant.io/?p=17432)
- [OPINION] Per-task leases are riskier for startups. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/robotics/robotics-as-a-service-raas)

**Direct vs channel in robotics**
- [OPINION] Do the first deployments yourself and add integrators at tens-to-hundreds of units. — [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/robotics_invest_panelists_discuss_startup_strategy_vc_criteria_in_boston/autonomy)
- [OPINION] Selling "direct, alone" is fading, and partner ecosystems (ABB, FANUC) are the norm. — [Robotics channel-mix analysis](https://cube-clownfish-lp7x.squarespace.com/artificial-intelligence-robotics/build-buy-or-partner-the-new-robotics-channel-mix)

**Venture client vs corporate VC**
- [OPINION/FACT] BMW explicitly positions itself as a customer, not an investor: founders keep equity, and the startup gets a supplier number and PO. This contrasts with CVC-led first deals. — [BMW press](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0426038EN/bmw-startup-garage-rolls-out-innovations-in-record-time); [BMW Startup Garage FAQ](https://www.bmwstartupgarage.com/en/faq.html)

### Inferences
- [INFERENCE] **Paid vs free.** In practice the debate resolves to "paid, with the fee credited toward conversion," and "free" only for tightly time-boxed technical feasibility. For robotics, where hardware and integration costs are real, unpaid pilots are an especially common route to pilot purgatory.
- [INFERENCE] **RaaS vs capex.** RaaS is easier to get the first customer to sign. Capex or financing through third-party lessors is easier for the startup's cash. A common middle path, seen in Formic offering both RaaS and financing, is to offer RaaS while moving the assets to third-party equipment-finance partners.
- [INFERENCE] **Vertical vs horizontal.** Every sourced robotics first-customer story here is a narrow vertical wedge: Gecko in power-plant boilers, Formic in SMB palletizing and packaging, Dexterity in parcel truck-loading, Agility in tote moving at a 3PL, Figure in an automotive plant. General-purpose humanoid companies still entered through one narrow task at one site. That supports "vertical wedge first," even for horizontal ambitions.
- [INFERENCE] **PLG for hardware** is credible only for developer and education hardware under about $1K (Reachy Mini). No evidence was found of PLG landing industrial robotics customers.

### Gaps
- No direct quotes were retrieved from YC, a16z, Lenny's, SaaStr, Eclipse, Lux, Playground Global or The Engine on these specific debates, including vertical vs horizontal and PLG for hardware. The searches that targeted a16z on pilots found no relevant a16z piece.
- No independent (non-vendor) data quantifies paid vs free pilot conversion or RaaS vs capex outcomes.
