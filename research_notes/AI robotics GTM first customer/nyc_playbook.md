Labels used: **[Fact]** = sourced fact; **[Opinion]** = expert/advocate opinion as quoted; **[Inference]** = researcher inference. Research date: 2026-11-07. ## 1. Dominant buyer industries and named organizations with AI/robotics pilots (2022–2026)

### Takeaway
The best-documented NYC robotics first-customer paths run through **commercial real estate owners and operators** (for example Durst with Skyline Robotics/Ozmo), **public agencies with structured pilot programs** (MTA, Port Authority, NYC DOT via Transit Tech Lab; NYCEDC Pilots at BAT; NYC DOT Innovation Studio with Newlab), and **public safety** (NYPD Digidog, Knightscope K5), which carries high reputational risk. Healthcare systems (Mount Sinai, Northwell) are big AI software buyers. This research found no documented NYC hospital robotics pilot.

### Cited Findings
**Real estate / property management**
- [Fact] Skyline Robotics (window-cleaning robot "Ozmo") raised a $6.5M pre-Series A in 2022 "ahead of first customer deployment of Ozmo in New York City." Investors included Kärcher New Venture and Gefen Capital. — [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/news/skyline-robotics-secures-6-5-million-in-pre-series-a-funding-ahead-of-first-customer-deployment-of-ozmo-window-cleaning-robot-in-new-york-city); [BusinessWire 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220322005502/en)
- [Fact] Skyline's go-to-market used a **channel partner**: Platinum, a building-maintenance firm, held exclusive rights to use Ozmo in New York. — [BusinessWire Sept 2022](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220927005553/en)
- [Fact] **Durst Ventures** (affiliate of The Durst Organization) invested in Skyline. In August 2024 Skyline, Durst and Palladium Window Solutions deployed Ozmo at 1133 Avenue of the Americas, a 45-story Durst tower. At that point it served one Midtown building and required a human operator. — [The Real Deal, Aug 2024](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/08/28/skylines-robotic-window-washer-debuts-at-durst-office-tower); [BSM Magazine](https://bsmmag.com/Articles/2024/Daily-Articles/08/Future-of-Window-Cleaning-Ozmo-Deployed-in-NYC.html)

It was retired or stored by early 2024. Reported cost was about $9/hour, and one headline cites a $12,250 total. — [ABC7 NY](https://abc7ny.com/post/nypd-robot-security-k5-subway-patrol/14381116/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/nyc-ends-trial-run-of-times-square-subways-security-robot-105528275.html); [amNY](https://www.amny.com/news/mayor-adams-nypd-deploy-robocop-for-2-month-pilot-in-times-square-subway-station); [Post Millennial](https://thepostmillennial.com/news/12250-robocop-out-of-commission-in-nyc-after-only-4-months)

In April 2023 Mayor Adams brought it back: about $750,000 for two units, paid with asset-forfeiture funds and restricted to life-threatening situations such as bomb threats. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/98284-nypd-brings-back-digidog-robot-dog-two-years.html); [Reason](https://reason.com/2023/04/12/new-york-city-brings-back-dystopian-robot-police-dogs/); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/nypds-spot-robot-is-back-for-use-in-hazardous-situations-061927023.html)

### Inferences
- [Inference] A landlord-led first deal is the most replicable NYC pattern. The Skyline/Durst case combined three things: a corporate-venture investment from the owner (Durst Ventures), an incumbent services contractor as channel (Platinum, then Palladium) and a single flagship building. Owners like Durst, Related, Brookfield, Tishman Speyer, Vornado and RXR are plausible targets, but this research found no documented 2023–2026 robotics pilots by Related, Brookfield, Tishman Speyer or Vornado.
- [Inference] Public-safety and surveillance-adjacent use cases (security robots, camera-heavy patrols) bring outsized political and press risk in NYC. Both NYPD robotics efforts drew sustained advocacy opposition, and the K5 was dropped after one pilot. Startups should position away from surveillance framing.
- [Inference] Hospitals are a plausible buyer for the AI software layer, which has documented demand. For robotics hardware, NYC systems appear less proven than upstate or national peers. Use Rochester or Cedars-style outcomes as reference proof.
- [Inference] Finance (J.P. Morgan as an NYR partner) is more likely a sponsor or investor than a robotics buyer, unless the use case is facilities or data-center operations.

### Gaps
- No verified 2023–2026 robotics pilots at NYC Health + Hospitals, Northwell or Mount Sinai.
- No verified pilots at Related, Brookfield, Tishman Speyer or Vornado. Did not cover retail, hospitality or logistics operators (e.g., Amazon or UPS facilities in NJ, grocery chains).
- The full 2025 and 2026 TTL company lists (to pick out robotics participants) were not retrieved because fetches were blocked.

## 2. Enterprise procurement norms, security reviews, sales cycles

### Takeaway
SOC 2 is a de facto requirement for enterprise software deals. Vendor-sourced data suggests deals without SOC 2 Type II take months longer (about 8.4 vs 5.0 months median, unverified). A Type I report in about 8–12 weeks is the common bridge for a first customer. No NYC-specific procurement data was found.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact, vendor-sourced/unverified] Drata's 2025 State of Trust report reportedly says companies with SOC 2 Type II closed enterprise deals 35% faster. Another analysis claims a median 8.4-month enterprise cycle without SOC 2 Type II vs 5.0 months with it. — [Technologymatch](https://technologymatch.com/blog/what-tech-vendors-must-know-about-concerns-about-data-protection); [Wolfia](https://cdn.wolfia.com/blog/soc-2-compliance-requirements-complete-guide)
- [Fact, vendor-sourced] SOC 2 Type II appears as mandatory or strongly preferred in about 87% of analyzed security questionnaires. Another figure: 83% of enterprise buyers require SOC 2 before signing. — [Rippling](https://www.rippling.com/blog/soc-2-for-startups); [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/resources/saas-security-report-2025)
- [Fact] SOC 2 is not legally required. Type I is a point-in-time assessment achievable in about 8–12 weeks. Type II needs an observation window of roughly 3–12 months. — [Promise Legal](https://promise.legal/guides/soc2-roadmap); [Petronella](https://petronellatech.com/blog/soc-2-for-saas-companies-from-zero-to-certified-in-90-days); [Comp AI](https://trycomp.ai/soc-2-before-series-a)
- [Fact] Public pilots through TTL use eight-week proofs of concept with agency partners, which shortens access to public buyers compared with formal procurement. — [Cities Today](https://cities-today.com/?p=72068)
- [Fact] NYPD's K5 was a short-term lease at about $9/hour rather than a purchase, which shows public buyers favor low-commitment pilot structures. — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/nyc-ends-trial-run-of-times-square-subways-security-robot-105528275.html)

### Inferences
- [Inference] For a combined hardware and software product, expect two parallel reviews: (a) InfoSec/vendor risk (SOC 2, data flows, camera and sensor data retention) and (b) facilities/EHS/insurance (UL/safety certifications, certificate of insurance, union impact). Start both in the first meeting.
- [Inference] Bridging tactics: offer a paid pilot scoped to one site, bound data residency, an on-prem or edge processing option, and a SOC 2 Type I letter plus a Type II timeline.

### Gaps
- No primary NYC-specific data on enterprise sales-cycle length, or on NYC City procurement (PPB rules, MWBE, small-purchase thresholds). These need follow-up, for example via the NYC Procurement Policy Board rules and the MOCS PASSPort vendor enrollment process.
- Did not verify typical insurance minimums (general liability or product liability) that NYC landlords demand of robot vendors.

## 3. Public programs: NYCEDC, NYC agencies, MTA, Port Authority, NY State, federal

### Takeaway
The most actionable non-dilutive or pilot channels are **Transit Tech Lab** (MTA, PANYNJ, NYC DOT), **NYC DOT x Newlab Innovation Studio**, **NYCEDC Pilots at BAT** (Brooklyn Army Terminal, expanding to MADE Bush Terminal), **Blue Highways / microhubs** (freight and last-mile), **Empire AI** (compute) and **NSF SBIR** (Phase I up to $305K, relaunched in 2026 after a reauthorization lapse).

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] **NYCEDC Pilots at BAT** launched in 2023 to let companies test technologies at Brooklyn Army Terminal before commercializing. In April 2025 it added three companies, including robotics firm Pliant Energy Systems, whose engineering director said the pilot would help it "showcase the groundbreaking capabilities that our robots bring for potential customers" in NYC. NYCEDC referenced expansion to MADE Bush Terminal. — [Brooklyn Reporter, Apr 2025](https://brooklynreporter.com/2025/04/three-new-companies-join-pilots-at-bat-program)
- [Fact] **Blue Highways** (NYCEDC and NYC DOT) aims to move freight by water. The Action Plan, released October 31, 2025, sets pilots at Pier 11 (later Downtown Skyport), Weehawken to Pier 79 (Hudson) and Brooklyn Marine Terminal to Governors Island. The first micro-freight landing at Downtown Skyport is slated for Q4 2027. — [NYCEDC](https://edc.nyc/node/7317); [Tribeca Citizen/eBroadsheet](https://www.ebroadsheet.com/blue-streak/); [CB4 letter, Feb 2026](https://cbmanhattan.cityofnewyork.us/cb4/wp-content/uploads/sites/10/2026/02/05-TPC-Letter-to-DOT-EDC-re-Blue-Highways-Pilot-Program-at-Pier-79.pdf)
- [Fact] NYC DOT's microhub pilot is planned to grow to 36 locations and integrate with Blue Highways. NYC authorized e-cargo bikes on city streets in March 2024. A 2026 DOT release reports reduced truck trips from microhubs. — [NYC DOT 2025](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2025/microhub-launch-amsterdam-ave.shtml); [NYC DOT pilot report](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/microhubs-pilot-report.pdf); [NYC DOT 2026](https://home4.nyc.gov/html/dot/html/pr2026/microhubs-reduced-truck-trips.shtml)
- [Fact] **Transit Tech Lab**: see Section 1. It is an annual cycle, with 2025 tracks in Ridership and Inspection & Maintenance and 2026 focused on infrastructure modernization. Partners are MTA, PANYNJ and NYC DOT. Proofs of concept run eight weeks, and successful companies can advance to longer pilots. — [Transit Innovation Partnership](https://transitinnovation.org/news/transit-tech-lab-releases-proof-of-concept-results-addressing-ridership-improvement-and-inspection-maintenance-challenges); [2025 TTL overview PDF](https://www.railforum.uk/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-TTL-Overview.pdf)
- [Fact] **Empire AI** is a public-private consortium of about $400M (up to $275M from the state, more than $125M from institutions). Founding members are Columbia, Cornell, NYU, CUNY, SUNY, RPI and the Flatiron Institute, with the supercomputer hosted at the University at Buffalo. The FY2026 budget added $90M from the state plus $25M from SUNY. — [Governor's office, Apr 2024](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-launches-empire-ai-consortium-make-new-york-global-leader-artificial); [CNYBJ](https://cnybj.com/new-state-budget-includes-funding-for-the-empire-ai-consortium/amp)
- [Fact] **NSF SBIR**: the Phase I cap rose from $275K to $305K. SBIR/STTR authorization lapsed for several months (late 2025 into 2026), and NSF paused Project Pitches. NSF relaunched under solicitation NSF 26-510, reportedly with a first full-proposal deadline of July 27, 2026 and a new "Strategic Breakthrough" award of up to $30M. Robotics is an NSF SBIR topic area, but its current open or closed status is unverified. Sources are mostly aggregators. — [GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/sbir-sttr-reauthorization-strategic-breakthrough-awards-small-business-innovation-2026); [GrantedAI (lapse)](https://grantedai.com/blog/sbir-sttr-reauthorization-lapse-2026-what-small-businesses-should-do); [Syracuse TunaBotics example](https://srg.syr.edu/srg-spinoff-tunabotics-receives-nsf-sbir-phase-i-funding/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] For a robotics + AI startup, TTL is the highest-signal public route: real agency operators, an eight-week PoC and a defined path to paid pilots. Inspection & Maintenance-type tracks fit robotics best.
- [Inference] Pilots at BAT gives a physical NYC test site for demos to prospective customers. That is valuable for hardware startups without their own floor space.
- [Inference] Blue Highways and microhubs create a near-term (2026–2027) opening for last-mile robotics and automation at waterfront freight landings, with NYC DOT and NYCEDC as the decision-makers.

### Gaps
- Could not verify NYCEDC "Futureworks" status (2023–2026), NYC Ferry robotics opportunities, or a dedicated Port Authority logistics innovation program beyond TTL.
- NYSERDA robotics or AI funding programs and Empire State Development startup grants were not found in this pass.
- No verified details on Empire AI compute access terms for startups.

## 4. NYC- and NY-specific regulation

### Takeaway
Most relevant to a robotics + AI product: (1) **NYC Local Law 144** if any AI touches hiring or promotion; (2) the **NY RAISE Act**, which targets frontier-model developers and is unlikely to apply directly to an early-stage startup; (3) **sidewalk and street rules**: motorized vehicles are barred from NYC sidewalks, and NYC DOT sent FedEx a cease-and-desist over its delivery robot in 2019. Building and fire code rules specific to robots were not found.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] **Local Law 144 (AEDT)** requires bias audits and notices for automated employment decision tools. A December 2, 2025 NY State Comptroller audit of DCWP enforcement (covering July 2023 to June 2025) found the complaint process does not reliably route complaints. Reviewers flagged at least 17 potential non-compliance issues across 32 bias audits, while DCWP had flagged one. The audit made 13 recommendations, and DCWP agreed to most. — [NY OSC audit](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools); [DCI summary](https://blog.dciconsult.com/recommended-changes-to-local-law-144)
- [Fact] **RAISE Act** (Responsible AI Safety and Education Act) was signed by Governor Hochul on December 19, 2025 and covers large frontier AI developers. Chapter amendments aligned it with California SB 53 and cut penalties to $1M/$3M, with an effective date of January 1, 2027. Sources conflict on the date the final narrowed version was signed (one says March 27, 2026). — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/governor-hochul-signs-new-yorks-ai-safety-act-220503930.html); [FPF](https://fpf.org/?p=247764); [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/legislation/new-york-raise-act); [ISOC summary](https://isoclive.substack.com/p/ny-raise-act)
- [Fact] **Sidewalk robots**: in 2019 NYC DOT sent FedEx a cease-and-desist over its SameDay Bot, citing vehicle and traffic rules, a state law restricting autonomous vehicles and the ban on motor vehicles on sidewalks. — [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/82943-new-york-tells-fedex-get-robot-off-city.html); [City & State](https://www.cityandstateny.com/articles/policy/technology/could-robots-ease-new-york-city-congestion.html)
- [Fact, low-quality source] A commercial blog claims NYC requires DOT permits for delivery robots, with limited pilots in 2025–2026. No official source corroborates this. — [GrabARobot blog](https://grabarobot.com/blog/last-mile-delivery-robot-regulations-2026/)
- [Fact] Separately, the city has drafted regulation of delivery apps, which concerns delivery workers rather than robots. — [Streetsblog, Feb 2025](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2025/02/10/deep-dive-city-hall-offers-glimpse-of-draft-plan-to-regulate-delivery-apps)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Indoor and private-property robots (in buildings, hospitals, warehouses, construction sites) face far lower regulatory friction than public-sidewalk robots. A first NYC customer should be indoors or on private property.
- [Inference] If the product's AI does workforce scheduling, performance scoring or hiring, Local Law 144 may apply to the customer (the employer). Expect buyers to ask for a bias audit or an exemption analysis.
- [Inference] Camera and sensor robots in public-facing spaces can trigger NYC biometric-identifier signage rules (Admin Code §22-1201ff, the 2021 commercial establishments biometric law). This was not verified in this pass.

### Gaps
- No verified robot-specific NYC Building Code or FDNY rules (e.g., robots in elevators or egress paths, lithium-battery charging; FDNY has e-bike battery rules that may be relevant).
- Did not verify NYC drone rules (NYPD/FAA; NYC Admin Code largely bans takeoff and landing outside designated sites), NY SHIELD Act data-security duties, or NY State AI workforce disclosure (WARN) changes.

## 5. Labor and union considerations

### Takeaway
32BJ SEIU has openly opposed window-cleaning robots in NYC and has raised automation in contract fights. Hospitality unions (UNITE HERE affiliates) have won technology-notice clauses of 30 to 180 days. Position robots as augmenting workers and safety tools, and engage unions early.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact/Opinion] 32BJ SEIU's NY Metro commercial division director publicly criticized Skyline's Ozmo, arguing that this is not the first time robots have been introduced to replace window cleaners. — [The Real Deal, Aug 2024](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/08/28/skylines-robotic-window-washer-debuts-at-durst-office-tower)
- [Fact] In 2025, 32BJ (Florida district) protested at Nova Southeastern University, saying a contractor's interest in exploring robots was part of its objection to a contract offer. — [WLRN](https://www.wlrn.org/business/2025-06-24/unionized-janitors-landscapers-rally-for-better-wages-against-robots-at-nova-southeastern)
- [Fact] 32BJ's NYC commercial cleaners contract (Dec 2023) has about 3% annual raises over four years. A residential tentative agreement with the Realty Advisory Board was reached in April 2026. Automation clauses were not confirmed in either. — [The Real Deal 2023](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2023/12/28/office-landlords-reach-deal-with-32bj-seiu-union-cleaners); [The Real Deal 2026](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2026/04/17/building-workers-reach-tentative-contract-agreement/)
- [Fact] Hospitality analog: the Las Vegas Culinary Union contract requires up to six months' notice before implementing technology, including AI, that affects members. The union says it does not restrict whether management adopts automation. UNITE HERE–Marriott contracts require 30 days' notice for changes to existing technology and 165 days for new technology, plus preferential rehire, severance and benefits for displaced workers. — [Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-union-tech-battles/734541/); [Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/in-vegas-casino-union-dispute-balancing-tech-and-a-personal-touch-was-key); [Bloomberg Tax](https://news.bloombergtax.com/delaware-brief/las-vegas-union-scores-ai-daily-cleaning-wins-in-caesars-pact)
- [Fact] Unions in the NJ market are adapting to AI and robotics becoming mainstream. — [NJBIZ](https://njbiz.com/unions-forced-to-adapt-as-artificial-intelligence-robotics-become-mainstream-subscriber-content/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] In unionized NYC buildings (32BJ), hotels (HTC) and transit (TWU Local 100), a robot that visibly replaces a union role will face resistance. Frame the product around high-risk or unfilled tasks (facade work, inspection at height, overnight hazmat), keep a human operator in the loop (as Ozmo did) and route deployment through the incumbent unionized contractor (as Skyline did with Platinum and Palladium).
- [Inference] Assume HTC contracts may include technology-notice provisions similar to other UNITE HERE agreements. Verify before targeting NYC hotels.

### Gaps
- HTC (NY Hotel and Gaming Trades Council) contract technology language was not found. No verified Teamsters (e.g., UPS 2023 contract tech provisions) or TWU Local 100 stance on robotics in this pass.

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

## 8. Deal size and pricing norms (USD)

### Takeaway
Robotics in this segment is typically sold as RaaS, priced per robot per month. Documented and reported anchors: security robots at about $2K–$10.5K per month, commercial cleaning robots at about $500–$3K per month, and fleet software at $50–$500 per robot per month. NYC public examples are small (K5 at about $9/hour, roughly $12K total; Digidog at $750K for two units purchased). Most figures are vendor or aggregator sourced.

### Cited Findings
- [Fact] NYPD K5 lease: about $9/hour, with one outlet citing $12,250. NYPD Digidog: $750K for two (2023), versus an earlier $94K contract (2020–21). — [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/nyc-ends-trial-run-of-times-square-subways-security-robot-105528275.html); [Post Millennial](https://thepostmillennial.com/news/12250-robocop-out-of-commission-in-nyc-after-only-4-months); [TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/98284-nypd-brings-back-digidog-robot-dog-two-years.html)
- [Fact, aggregator] Knightscope is about $4,500 per robot per month (about $54K/year) for 24/7 patrol, citing IPVM. A Cobalt enterprise example is about $126K/year. Security robot leases range about $2K–$9K/month, or $70K–$300K to buy and deploy. — [robotics.press 2026](https://robotics.press/news/security-robot-pricing-2026/); [Alibaba/IPVM citation](https://electronics.alibaba.com/supplier/knightscope-security-robot-price)
- [Fact] Dax security robots: hourly patrol rate plus a monthly location fee, about $3.4K–$9.1K/month. — [Dax pricing](https://daxbot.com/security-robots/pricing)
- [Fact, aggregator] Cleaning robots: SoftBank Whiz lease about $499/month; LionsBot $1,000–$1,600/month; Avidbots rental $2,500–$3,000/month. Fleet software runs $50–$500 per robot per month. Industrial arms and AMRs on RaaS run $1,500–$8,000 per robot per month. — [Keyi Robotics](https://keyirobot.com/blogs/insights/service-robot-cost); [Robotomated](https://robotomated.com/learn/market/robotics-as-a-service-raas-guide); [Compound RaaS model](https://businessmodels.compound.vc/model/raas)
- [Fact] Hospital robotics: Rochester Regional pays for Moxi through a subscription tied to how much the robots work (usage-based). — [Rochester Regional](https://www.rochesterregional.org/hub/moxi-robot-rgh-unity-hospital)

### Inferences
- [Inference] A realistic first NYC paid deal for an AI + robotics startup is a single-site paid pilot of about $25K–$150K over 3–6 months (one to a few units on RaaS plus a software fee), with a conversion clause to a 12–36 month RaaS contract. This is derived from the monthly RaaS ranges above and has not been observed directly in NYC data.
- [Inference] Usage- or outcome-based pricing (per hour, per task, per delivery) lowers buyer risk and fits public pilot norms (the K5 hourly lease) and hospital norms (the Moxi utilization subscription).

### Gaps
- No verified NYC-specific enterprise deal sizes for robotics or AI pilots in real estate or healthcare. No audited RaaS pricing benchmarks; most numbers come from vendors or aggregators.
