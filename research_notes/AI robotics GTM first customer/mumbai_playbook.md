## 1. Price sensitivity, deal sizes, RaaS models and labour-cost ROI in India

### Takeaway
Very few Indian robotics companies publish prices. The best public anchors are municipal purchases of Genrobotics' Bandicoot (about ₹15–39 lakh, or US$17k–44k, per unit, sold outright or rented) and RaaS-plus-purchase offerings from AMR makers such as Ati Motors (now Ati Robotics). Unskilled wages in Maharashtra are roughly ₹13,600–15,000 per month (about US$155–170). That makes single-shift labour-replacement ROI weak. Payback improves with multi-shift operation, safety or hazard cases, throughput or quality gains, and leasing or RaaS that turns capex into opex.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact]** Genrobotics Bandicoot (sewer-cleaning robot) is reported at **₹15–35 lakh (about US$17k–40k)**, sold or rented. Actual municipal buys were about **₹39 lakh (about US$44k)** in Udaipur and Mysuru. Build cost is reported at about **₹32 lakh (Rs 3.2 million, about US$36k)** per unit. — [Mooknayak](https://en.themooknayak.com/dalit-news/bandicoot-robots-revolutionize-sanitation-in-rajasthan-ending-manual-sewerage-hazards); [Deccan Herald (Mysuru)](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/now-robot-to-help-clean-underground-drainage-in-mysuru-945464.html); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/asia/india/robot-on-a-mission-to-eliminate-manual-cleaning-of-sewers-1.1568196597101)
- **[Fact, low-confidence secondary]** One report gives an entry price of **₹15 lakh on a 24-month lease, excluding maintenance**, for a single Bandicoot. Treat with caution. — [RobotWale](https://robotwale.com/news/genrobotic-bandicoot-scavenger)
- **[Fact]** Zoho invested in Genrobotics. The company sells mainly to municipal and government bodies. — [Zoho](https://prezohoweb.zoho.com/news/zoho-invests-in-genrobotics.html)
- **[Fact]** Ati Motors raised **US$20M (about ₹176 crore)** in January 2025. It offers both **RaaS (lease) and outright purchase** of its Sherpa AMRs. Customers include Hyundai Motor India. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/22/ati-motors-raises-20m-as-indias-robotics-industry-grows/); [Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/resource/hyundai_motor_india_successfully_deploys_sherpa_robots_from_ati_motors)
- **[Fact, secondary]** Ati's Sherpa Mecha humanoid has a stated price of **US$80,000 (about ₹70 lakh)**. The company reportedly renamed itself **Ati Robotics in April 2026**. — [Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/welcome-sherpa-mecha/); search summary of [indianaiapps](https://www.indianaiapps.com/apps/ati-motors)
- **[Fact, single secondary source]** Addverb (55% owned by Reliance Retail after a **US$132M** investment in 2021) reported FY25 revenue of about **₹334 crore (about US$38M)** with about 1,876 employees. A separate "₹800 crore" figure is a target, not an actual. — [Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2025/10/09/top-30-indian-robotics-companies-competition-foreign-and-domestic/95313/)
- **[Fact]** Addverb built an automated grocery warehouse for Reliance. This is a group-captive anchor-customer model. — [Addverb case study](https://addverb.com/warehouse-automation-case-studies/reliance-automated-grocery-warehouse/)
- **[Fact, secondary]** GreyOrange, founded in India and now based in Atlanta, serves Walmart, IKEA and Apple globally. Its India business is mostly sortation for large e-commerce hubs. — [Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2025/10/09/top-30-indian-robotics-companies-competition-foreign-and-domestic/95313/)
- **[Fact]** Maharashtra unskilled minimum wage: Zone I is about **₹14,317 per month (about US$163)** from January 2025 (₹10,021 basic + ₹3,614 VDA + ₹681.75 HRA). It is about **₹13,635 per month** under the July 2025 revision. Other schedules give up to about ₹14,956. Sources conflict by schedule. — [Ascent HR (Feb 2025 revision)](https://ascent-hr.com/?p=32037); [Ascent HR (July 2025)](https://ascent-hr.com/notification/minimum-wages-effective-from-july-2025/); [Keka](https://www.keka.com/library/minimum-wages/maharashtra)
- **[Fact, analyst]** Large brownfield campuses in **Bhiwandi** and Sriperumbudur use high-bay AS/RS to roughly quadruple pallet density within existing footprints. In this framing, land and space savings, not just labour, drive automation ROI in MMR. — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/indian-warehouse-automation-market)
- **[Fact, IndiaAI compute pricing as a software-cost anchor]** IndiaAI subsidised GPU compute is quoted at about **₹65–92 per GPU-hour (about US$0.74–1.05)**. The minister has cited a low of ₹67. — [TechDodo](https://techdodo.in/articles/india-ai-impact-summit-2026-gpu-access-guide); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fmodi-government-eyes-indias-deepseek-in-6-10-months-3381635)

### Inferences
- **[Inference] Rough ROI math.** One unskilled worker costs about ₹14k per month in wages, or about ₹1.7 lakh (US$1.9k) a year. Adding statutory PF, ESI, bonus and contractor margin (an assumed 25–35%) brings this to about ₹2.1–2.3 lakh. A ₹25–35 lakh robot replacing one person on one shift pays back in more than 10 years. On three shifts (about 3 FTEs, roughly ₹6.5–7 lakh a year), payback is about 4–5 years. That is still long for Indian CFOs, who often expect 2–3 years. So the first-customer pitch should lean on **safety or hazard work** (the Bandicoot model), **throughput, uptime or quality**, **space density** (Bhiwandi land costs), **labour availability and attrition**, and **multinational or export-customer compliance**, not headcount savings alone.
- **[Inference]** For an early-stage startup, a **pilot fee plus RaaS** structure fits Indian buyer psychology better than capex: an opex line, the vendor carries performance risk, and it is easier to approve below capex-committee thresholds. A possible structure is a paid PoC of ₹5–25 lakh (US$6k–28k), then a monthly per-robot or per-pick fee. The ₹25 lakh MSInS pilot cap below is a useful public-sector benchmark for "pilot-sized" tickets.
- **[Inference]** Group-captive anchoring (Addverb–Reliance) shows that the biggest Indian robotics outcomes came from a conglomerate both investing in and buying from the startup. A strategic-investor-plus-customer route is a realistic first-customer path.

### Gaps
- No public INR price lists or deal sizes were found for Addverb, GreyOrange, CynLr, Niqo Robotics, Sastra, Peer Robotics, Unbox Robotics, Haber, Detect Technologies, Gnani.ai, Sarvam AI or Yellow.ai. These would need company interviews, tender documents on GeM or CPPP, or MCA filings via Tofler.
- No audited, India-specific robotics payback studies were found (the payback search returned nothing).
- Ati Sherpa RaaS monthly pricing is not published.

In August 2025 it launched an accelerator that names robotics and automation as a focus. Reliance's model is to acquire or invest and then buy captively (Addverb). Adani now controls both Mumbai airports, with Navi Mumbai airport operating since 25 December 2025. ## 3. Mumbai / Maharashtra specifics: MMR logistics, MSInS, startup policy, BMC, MIDC

### Takeaway
Maharashtra's Startup, Entrepreneurship and Innovation Policy 2025 (approved August 2025, implemented by the Maharashtra State Innovation Society, MSInS) offers the most direct state-level first-customer route. Startups chosen through the **Maharashtra Startup Week** can get **government pilot work orders of up to ₹25 lakh (about US$28k)**, and each department earmarks **0.5% of its annual outlay** for innovation. Bhiwandi (warehousing), JNPA (port) and Navi Mumbai airport are the main logistics demand nodes.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact]** The Maharashtra Startup, Entrepreneurship and Innovation Policy 2025 was approved by the state cabinet in **August 2025**. It is implemented by **MSInS** as the apex body, with targets of **1.25 lakh entrepreneurs and 50,000 recognised startups** over 5 years. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/maharashtra-cabinet-unveils-startup-policy-to-nurture-entrepreneurs/2714236/); [Drishti IAS](https://www.drishtiias.com/state-pcs-current-affairs/maharashtra-startup-entrepreneurship-and-innovation-policy-2025)
- **[Fact]** Startups selected via **Maharashtra Startup Week** (about 50 a year through a rigorous selection) can work with government departments and get **pilot work orders of up to ₹25 lakh**. Each department earmarks **0.5% of its annual outlay** for innovation and entrepreneurship. — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/maharashtra-cabinet-unveils-startup-policy-to-nurture-entrepreneurs/2714236/); [Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/maharashtra-startup-entrepreneurship-innovation-policy-2025-011-1447633.html); [policy PDF mirror (partial)](https://amplinxt.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/MAHARASHTRA-STARTUP-ENTREPRENEURSHIP-AND-INNOVATION-POLICY-2025-1.pdf)

## 4. Government channels (national)

### Takeaway
The most actionable national channels for a first sale are: (a) **DPIIT recognition**, which unlocks GeM and public-procurement exemptions from prior turnover, prior experience and EMD; (b) **GeM Startup Runway**, which lists innovative products without needing past orders; (c) **iDEX**, defence challenge grants of about ₹1.5 crore, iDEX Prime up to ₹10 crore and ADITI up to ₹25 crore, which effectively fund a prototype for a named defence buyer; and (d) **IndiaAI** subsidised compute for the AI side. The RDI scheme (₹1 lakh crore) funds TRL-4+ deep tech through loans and equity, not grants. The National Strategy on Robotics is still a 2023 draft.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact, official]** Public procurement norms exempt startups from **prior experience, prior turnover and EMD**, subject to meeting quality and technical specifications. — [PIB, Ministry of Commerce](https://pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1565237)
- **[Fact, secondary]** Zero-turnover startups can bid under **GFR 2017 Rule 173** if technically compliant. The relaxation **applies only if the buyer enables the startup clause** in the bid, so it is not automatic. — [echai.ventures](https://echai.ventures/startingup/selling-to-government/i-m-a-dpiit-recognised-startup-what-procurement-relaxations-do-i-actually-get)
- **[Fact, secondary]** As of 10 October 2025, **34,000+ DPIIT-recognised startups** were on GeM, with orders of more than **₹46,000 crore (about US$5.2B)**. GeM Startup Runway needs a DPIIT certificate and a working, demonstrable product, but no turnover, experience or past government orders. — [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/startups/govt-procurement-portal-gem-eyes-to-onboard-1-lakh-startups-official-3211006); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/gem-startup-runway-revolutionizes-public-procurement-empowering-indias-innovators/2054641); [Professional Utilities](https://www.professionalutilities.com/startup-runway-on-gem)
- **[Fact]** **IndiaAI Mission**: a 5-year outlay of **₹10,372 crore (about US$1.18B)**. Subsidised GPU access is quoted at about ₹65–92 per GPU-hour, with up to a 40% subsidy. Deployed GPU counts are reported at about 34,000–45,000 by mid-2026 (sources conflict). DPIIT startups, academia and government agencies are eligible. — [TechDodo](https://techdodo.in/articles/india-ai-impact-summit-2026-gpu-access-guide); [TechPillow](https://www.techpillow.co/blog/indiaai-mission-38000-gpus-shakti-cloud-compute-2026); [Careerindia](https://www.careerindia.com/news/indiaai-mission-2026-access-subsidized-gpus-boost-ai-careers-scale-startups-011-66885.html) (all secondary)
- **[Fact]** **RDI Scheme**: approved by the Cabinet on **1 July 2025** with a **₹1 lakh crore (about US$11.4B)** outlay. DST is the nodal department, and the fund is housed in a Special Purpose Fund within **ANRF**. It provides long-term low or zero-interest loans, equity (mainly for startups) and a Deep-Tech Fund of Funds, with **no grants**. It targets **TRL 4+**. One source says ₹20,000 crore (about US$2.3B) is allocated for FY26. — [DST RDI Cell](https://dst.gov.in/rdi-scheme/research-development-and-innovation-rdi-cell); [India Science & Technology](https://www.indiascienceandtechnology.gov.in/stihighlights/%E2%82%B91-lakh-crore-push-research-and-innovation-under-rdi-scheme); [Rau's IAS](https://compass.rauias.com/current-affairs/research-development-innovation-rdi-scheme/)
- **[Unverified claim]** One coaching site says fresh RDI applications were **paused as of September 2026**. This could not be confirmed against an official source. — [Superkalam](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/articles/rdi-fund-rs-1-lakh-crore-deep-tech-funding-and-application-pause)
- **[Fact, official PDF]** **iDEX ADITI** offers grants of up to **50% of the product development budget, capped at ₹25 crore (about US$2.8M)**, paid by milestone. The sub-scheme budget is **₹750 crore (about US$85M)** for FY2023-24 to FY2025-26. — [iDEX ADITI scheme PDF](https://idex.gov.in/uploads/page/aditi_scheeme.pdf)
- **[Fact, secondary]** Standard **iDEX grants go up to ₹1.5 crore (about US$170k)** per challenge winner and **iDEX Prime up to ₹10 crore (about US$1.14M)**. ADITI 4.0 had 25 problem statements and closed on 4 May 2026. — [startupfunds.in](https://startupfunds.in/schemes/idex-innovations-defence-excellence); [StartupGrantsIndia](https://www.startupgrantsindia.com/aditi-4-0-challenge-grant-program-defence-innovation-organisation-idex-422)
- **[Fact]** The **Draft National Strategy on Robotics** (MeitY) was open for comment from 4 September to 31 October 2023. It aims to make India a robotics leader by 2030, focused on **manufacturing, healthcare, agriculture and national security**. It proposes a two-tier **National Robotics Mission** and a **Robotics Innovation Unit under MeitY / IndiaAI**. No finalisation or 2025 update was found. — [MyGov Innovate India](https://innovateindia.mygov.in/national-strategy-on-robotics/); [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/meity-releases-draft-national-strategy-for-robotics-key-highlights/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Recommended order: get DPIIT recognition first (it is a prerequisite for GeM relaxations, Startup Runway and IndiaAI compute), then a Startup Runway listing, then target iDEX open challenges that match the robot's capability (inspection, logistics, hazardous handling). An iDEX win comes with a named defence end-user and a path to procurement.
- **[Inference]** RDI money is loans and equity at TRL 4+, routed through fund managers. It is a later-stage financing channel, not a first-customer channel.

## 5. Import duties, GST, BIS and local assembly vs import

### Takeaway
Industrial robots (tariff item 8479 50 00) appear to carry **7.5% Basic Customs Duty plus a 10% Social Welfare Surcharge on BCD plus IGST** (18% is most commonly cited; the post-2025 GST rationalisation rate is unclear). That gives about a 27.7–28.5% landed tax load. Most IGST is recoverable as input tax credit, so the real cost is closer to 8–9% plus working capital. Sources are third-party tariff sites. Verify on CBIC ICEGATE.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact, secondary tariff aggregators]** HS **8479 50 00** covers "industrial robots not elsewhere specified". BCD is **7.5%**, with SWS of 0.75 points. Total effective duty including IGST is about **27.7%** (Treayo) or **28.47%** (Eximpe) on CIF. — [Eximpe 84795000](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/84795000); [Treayo](https://treayo.com/en/hs/india/84795000); [Seair](https://www.seair.co.in/custom-duty/hs-code-84795000.aspx)
- **[Fact, conflicting]** The GST rate on 8479 50 00 is listed as **18%** by most sources. One cites IGST rate notification **9/2025** with 5% or 18% depending on the goods. — [Busy.in](https://busy.in/hsn/hsn-84795000/); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/hsn-code/84795000)
- **[Fact, secondary]** India imported about **₹1,665 crore (about US$189M)** under 8479 50 00 in FY2024-25. — [Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/84795000)
- **[Fact]** Several sites mislabel 8428 70 00 as "industrial robots". 8428 is lifting and handling machinery, so classify AMRs and AGVs carefully. — [Eximpe 84287000](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/84287000)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Worked example: a ₹20 lakh CIF robot arm. BCD 7.5% = ₹1.5 lakh, SWS = ₹0.15 lakh, IGST at 18% on ₹21.65 lakh = ₹3.9 lakh. Landed cost is about ₹25.5 lakh. If the importer is GST-registered and resells or uses the robot in a taxable business, the ₹3.9 lakh IGST is creditable, so the non-recoverable cost is about ₹1.65 lakh (8.25%).
- **[Inference]** Local assembly in India (importing sub-assemblies such as motors, drives, LiDAR and compute, with in-house integration and software) supports "Make in India" positioning. That helps with GeM, Class-I local-supplier preference under the Public Procurement (Make in India) order, and defence buyers. Customs on components may be similar or lower, but this was not verified per component.

### Gaps
- **BIS certification**: no source found on whether robots, AMRs, robot controllers or battery packs fall under BIS Compulsory Registration (CRS) or a Quality Control Order. Lithium battery packs and some electronics/IT products are under CRS (background knowledge, unverified). This needs a check on bis.gov.in and the latest machinery/electrical QCOs (a "Machinery and Electrical Equipment Safety" omnibus QCO was proposed; status unverified).
- WPC/ETA approval for wireless modules (Wi-Fi, 5G, LoRa) in robots was not researched.
- Official CBIC confirmation of the BCD and IGST rates is missing.

## 7. Payment terms, collections risk and contract enforcement

### Takeaway
Since 1 April 2024, **Section 43B(h)** of the Income Tax Act has disallowed a buyer's tax deduction for amounts owed to **registered micro or small enterprises** that go unpaid beyond **15 days (no agreement) or 45 days (written agreement)**. The deduction comes back only in the year of payment. This is real leverage for a startup that registers on **Udyam** as micro or small and tells buyers so. Medium enterprises are not covered.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact]** Section 43B(h) was inserted by the Finance Act 2023 and has been effective since **1 April 2024**. Payments to registered micro and small suppliers are due within 15 days, or within 45 days with a written agreement. Otherwise the deduction is allowed only in the year of actual payment. **Medium enterprises are excluded.** Suppliers must **inform buyers of their MSME (Udyam) registration.** — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/45-day-msme-payment-rule-impact-and-details-of-section-43b-h-explained-124032600333_1.html); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/payment-msme-45-15-days-section-43b-h-explained.html); [Vayana](https://vayana.com/blogs/vayana-explains-section-43b-h-ensure-timely-payments-for-msmes/)
- **[Opinion/contested]** Some commentators say contracts cannot extend MSME payment beyond 45 days at all. Others call that a stronger reading than the statute supports. Industry bodies have lobbied both for and against keeping the rule, so it remains under debate. — [SetIndiaBiz](https://www.setindiabiz.com/blog/45-day-msme-payment-rule); [Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/45-day-msme-payment-rule-impact-and-details-of-section-43b-h-explained-124032600333_1.html)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Action items: register on Udyam (micro or small classification), print the Udyam number on quotes and invoices, write 45-day terms into the PO or MSA, and invoice by milestone (for example, 30–40% advance on PO, 40–50% on delivery and commissioning, 10–20% on acceptance). For RaaS, bill monthly in advance. Large Indian corporates often default to 60–90+ day terms. Section 43B(h) is the lever to push back. Note: 43B(h) concerns the buyer's *tax deduction*. It is not a collections mechanism.
- **[Inference]** For non-payment, the MSMED Act's Facilitation Council route (via the **MSME Samadhaan** portal) gives micro and small suppliers compound interest at 3x the RBI bank rate and conciliation or arbitration. That is generally faster than civil courts, where commercial contract enforcement in India is slow. *MSMED Act mechanics are background knowledge, not sourced this pass.*

### Gaps
- MSME Samadhaan statistics (cases filed, amounts pending, resolution rates) were not found.
- No sourced data on actual payment-term norms at Tata, Reliance or other conglomerates, or on World Bank or other contract-enforcement timelines for Mumbai commercial courts.

## 8. Data / privacy and AI governance

### Takeaway
The **DPDP Rules 2025** were notified on **13 November 2025**, with a phased rollout. The Data Protection Board took effect immediately, Consent Manager rules apply from **13 November 2026**, and the substantive obligations (notice, consent, rights, security, breach notification) apply from about **13 May 2027**. The **India AI Governance Guidelines** (5 November 2025) are non-binding and pro-innovation, rely on existing law, and are not an AI statute. For a robotics-plus-AI vendor, the near-term compliance load is moderate. Enterprise buyers will increasingly ask for DPDP-aligned data processing agreements before May 2027.

### Cited Findings
- **[Fact]** The DPDP Rules were notified on **13 November 2025**. Rules 1, 2 and 17–21, including establishment of the Data Protection Board, took effect immediately. **Rule 4 (Consent Managers) applies from 13 November 2026.** **Rules 3, 5–16, 22 and 23** (notice, rights, security safeguards, breach notification and more) **apply from 13 May 2027**, an 18-month horizon. One firm gives 12 May 2027 instead. — [AZB & Partners](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/indias-digital-personal-data-protection-act-phased-rollout-and-key-compliance-milestones/); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2025/11/digital-personal-data-protection-dpdp-rules-2025-rollout-over-18-months); [Grant Thornton PDF](https://www.grantthornton.in/globalassets/1.-member-firms/india/assets/pdfs/flyers/dpdp-rules-nov-2025.pdf)
- **[Fact]** The **India AI Governance Guidelines** were released by MeitY on **5 November 2025** under the IndiaAI Mission. They set out **7 guiding principles ("Sutras")**, **6 pillars** (infrastructure, capacity building, policy and regulation, risk mitigation, accountability, institutions) and a short-, medium- and long-term action plan. They prefer existing laws, and say AI training on personal data must comply with DPDP. They propose an **AI Governance Group** plus a **Technology & Policy Expert Committee**. They are **not a statute**, and as of December 2025 India was not planning one. They follow a January 2025 draft. — [NeGD/MeitY press release](https://negd.gov.in/press_release/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines-under-indiaai-mission-to-ensure-safe-inclusive-and-responsible-adoption-of-artificial-intelligence-across-sectors/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/new-technology/1704828/meity-releases-india-ai-governance-guidelines-with-deliberate-focus-on-enabling-innovation-over-restraint); [SS Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/meity-unveils-india-ai-governance-guidelines/)

### Inferences
- **[Inference]** Robots with cameras in warehouses, plants, airports or public spaces capture worker and visitor images, which are personal data. Build privacy by design now (on-device processing, face blurring, retention limits, clear notices) and offer a DPA template. Enterprise procurement will ask for these ahead of May 2027, and DPDP penalties can reach ₹250 crore per breach (the Act's schedule; background knowledge).
- **[Inference]** The AI guidelines bring no licensing burden. Positioning the product as aligned with the "Sutras" (transparency, "understandable by design") is cheap marketing for government and PSU buyers.

### Gaps
- Sector rules were not researched: DGCA or BCAS rules for robots at airports, PESO and Factories Act rules for robots in hazardous zones, and the Factories Act or ISO 10218 / ISO 3691-4 safety standards as adopted by BIS.
