## 1. US competitors selling used/refurbished robots, their price levels and value-add

### Takeaway
The US used-robot market is mature and price-transparent: domestic dealers (RobotWorx, Robots Done Right, others) and marketplaces (eBay, Machinio, Surplus Record) list mid-size FANUC arc/handling robots at roughly $13k–$23k and large 165–210 kg arms at roughly $7k–$40k, often with warranty and US-voltage, US-supported configurations. A Spanish-sourced robot must beat these landed prices after freight, duty and compliance work, or compete on model availability (ABB/KUKA/Staubli variants scarcer in US inventory).

### Inferences
- European asking prices (e.g., KUKA KR 210 from ~€3k–€30k) can sit below US asking prices for comparable large arms, creating an arbitrage window—but the spread on a $15k–$30k robot is easily consumed by ~$3k–$8k of freight/crating/brokerage plus duties and any electrical/compliance rework (see section 3).
- Best fit for Eurobots stock in the US: (a) models/brands under-supplied in US used inventory (KUKA, ABB, Staubli, Kawasaki variants), (b) multi-unit lots that amortize freight, (c) late-generation controllers (R-30iB, IRC5, KR C4) which command premiums.
- US competitors' main value-add is low risk: domestic stock, warranty, test-run video, local service, US voltage. An agent selling imported units needs to match on warranty and pre-shipment testing documentation.

## 2. Typical used-robot price ranges, dealer margins and referral commissions; is 10% standard?

### Takeaway
Used industrial-equipment brokers typically take ~5–15% of sale price (lower % on higher-value items), so a 10% referral/finder's fee on a $15k–$40k robot is within normal range—roughly $1,500–$4,000 per unit. Dealer markup data specific to used robots was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Machinery broker commissions commonly cited at 5–15% of sale price depending on equipment type, complexity and market — [Ironmart Online, 2026](https://www.ironmartonline.com/standard-commission-rates-for-machinery-brokers-in-2026/)
- Another source: equipment broker commissions nationally typically 6–12%; heavy equipment 8–12%; low-value units (e.g., $10k item) can be 18–20% — [Grizz Equip](https://www.grizzequip.com/how-much-do-equipment-brokers-charge/)
- Tiered example: one broker charges 10% on smaller units down to 5% on equipment over $150,000 — [HDD Broker FAQ](https://www.hddbroker.com/en/listequipment/help/faq.php)
- Common "floor price" model: broker markets at retail and keeps anything above seller's minimum — [Grizz Equip](https://www.grizzequip.com/how-much-do-equipment-brokers-charge/)
- Example contract: finder's fee of 10% of net value of goods sold as a direct result of an introduction (lumber processing equipment) — [idrywood broker terms](https://idrywood.com/used-equipment-broker-terms)
- Gross vs net basis matters: "net" commission calculated after certain deductions — [Ironmart](https://www.ironmartonline.com/standard-commission-rates-for-machinery-brokers-in-2026/)

### Inferences
- 10% referral is market-standard-to-generous for a pure introduction with no inventory, import or warranty risk. It is in line with the 10% finder's-fee precedent.
- Key contract points for the agent: commission on gross invoice (ex-works price) vs net; whether it applies to repeat orders from the same customer; payment timing (on Eurobots' receipt of payment); non-circumvention clause; duration (e.g., 12–24 months tail).
- Reseller (markup) model: agent becomes importer of record or arranges DDP, bears FX (EUR/USD), freight damage, duty, compliance and warranty risk. Given US asking prices vs EU asking prices above, a reseller might target 20–35% gross markup over Eurobots ex-works price to cover ~$3k–$8k landed costs and profit — this is an inference, not a sourced benchmark.

### Gaps
- No sourced figure for typical used-robot dealer gross margins in the US.
- No Eurobots-specific agent/commission terms found publicly.

## 3. Import considerations Spain→US (freight, tariffs, customs, electrical, safety/compliance, warranty, licences)

### Takeaway
As of Nov 2026, industrial robots (HTS 8479.50.00) carry a 2.5% MFN duty; a Section 232 robotics/machinery tariff was reportedly NOT imposed (Oct 28, 2026 proclamation, review due ~April 2027); EU goods face a Section 301 replacement surcharge after Section 122 expired July 24, 2026, with sources conflicting on whether the EU all-in rate is 10% or 15%. Freight for a crated robot+controller likely runs a few thousand dollars per shipment. The bigger hurdles are US electrical code acceptance (NFPA 79 / NRTL field evaluation), voltage/frequency checks, and OEM software licence/support transfer.

### Cited Findings
**Tariffs / duties**
- HTS 8479.50.00.00 "Industrial robots, nesoi": general (MFN) duty 2.5% (2026 Rev. 22) — [HTSHub](https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/detail/8479500000); [Gatewaylines](https://tariff.gatewaylines.com/hts/8479500000)
- One table shows EU origin all-in 10% (2.5% MFN + 7.5% Section 301) for 8479.50 — UNVERIFIED single source — [Open-Factory](https://open-factory.com/tariffs/8479-50-00/)
- Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026; administration replaced them with a Section 122 global surcharge — [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/supreme-court-strikes-down-ieepa-tariffs-trump-responds-with-section-122-global-surcharge/); [Global Trade Alert](https://globaltradealert.org/blog/from-ieepa-to-section-122)
- CIT ruled against Section 122 tariffs May 7, 2026, but ruling stayed by Federal Circuit pending appeal — [Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/05/us-trade-court-strikes-down-section-122-tariffs)
- Section 122 expired 12:01 a.m. ET July 24, 2026 and new Section 301 duties of 10% or 12.5% on 60 economies took effect simultaneously; EU in the lower tier — [Torres Trade Law](https://www.torrestradelaw.com/posts/Global-Tariffs,-Repackaged:-USTR-Announces-New-Section-301-Tariffs-on-a-Broad-Range-of-Countries/448); [ustariffcalc](https://ustariffcalc.com/section-122-expired-what-replaces-it.html)
- An Oct 8, 2026 page says most EU goods pay a Section 301 duty bringing the total (incl. Column 1) to 10%; lines already ≥10% pay nothing extra — [ustariffrates.com EU](https://ustariffrates.com/tariff-rates/eu); CONFLICT: another guide says EU goods moved to a 15% all-inclusive ceiling from July 1, 2026 — [tariffstool.com](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-122-tariff-rates-2026)
- Section 232 robotics & industrial machinery: reportedly the President declined to impose tariffs; Oct 28, 2026 proclamation directs Commerce/USTR to update within 180 days (~Apr 2027) — [STR Trade Report headline](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/october/no-section-232-tariffs-on-robotics-and-industrial-machinery) (full text not fetched; summarized from search). Earlier trackers had described an EU 15% cap for Section 232 — [Wiley tariff tracker](https://www.wiley.law/trump-administration-tariff-tracker); [Dimerco](https://dimerco.com/us-tariff-update-2026/)
- Separate June 2026 Section 232 metals-derivative changes: Annex III fixed industrial machinery pays 15% combined with Column 1; reduced 10–15% machinery rate expires Dec 31, 2027 — [International Trade Insights, June 2026](https://www.internationaltradeinsights.com/2026/06/president-trump-reduces-section-232-tariffs-on-certain-agricultural-mobile-industrial-and-hvac-equipment/); [SEKO advisory](https://www.sekologistics.com/en/resource-hub/news/client-advisory-presidential-proclamation-reduces-section-232-rates-on-key-equipment-categories/) — whether robots (8479.50) fall under any metals-derivative annex was not confirmed.
- Harbor Maintenance Fee 0.125% of cargo value; US customs entry ~$150–$300 — [Freight-Calculator 2026](https://blog.freight-calculator.com/20-ft-container-shipping-cost-2026)

**Freight**
- 20ft container internationally in 2026: $2,500–$13,000 broad range; another guide: base ocean $1,200–$2,800 + fuel surcharge $250–$600; terminal/docs/customs/inland add $800–$2,500+ per container — [FreightAmigo](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/logistics/free-container-shipping-cost-calculator-accurate-rates-for-20ft-containers/); [Freight-Calculator](https://blog.freight-calculator.com/20-ft-container-shipping-cost-2026)

- A Bilbao→US 20ft FCL ~$3,650 appears on a forwarder page but is ~620 days old and template-like (low reliability) — [BR Logistics](https://www.brlogistics.net/us/ship-a-container/to-united-states/)

**Electrical / safety compliance**
- A machine built to EN 60204-1 would not comply with NFPA 79 and "could not be installed in North America" as-is; NFPA 79 is more detailed — [Certifico NFPA 79 doc](https://certifico.com/component/attachments/download/2338)
- CE mark not recognized as substitute for an NRTL mark; equipment needs NRTL listing or field evaluation; relevant standards for robot systems: UL 1740, NFPA 79, UL 508A — [Machine Safety Specialists](https://www.machinesafetyspecialists.com/nrtl-services/); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en-us/industries/manufacturing/components-and-equipment/electrical-safety)
- Field labeling requirements vary by local jurisdiction (AHJ) — [IAEI Magazine](https://iaeimagazine.org/2004/november2004/certification-of-factory-automation-equipment/)
- NFPA 79 nameplate must show voltage, phase, frequency, FLA, SCCR; missing nameplate with >5,000 A available fault current triggers costly field evaluation — [Red Safety IDs](https://redsafetyids.com/feeds/blog/machine-nameplate-requirements)
- Wiring color differences (e.g., neutral white US vs blue EU) and English-language labeling required — [Lewis Bass](https://lewisbass.com/ask-lewis-bass-nfpa-79-compliance-for-industrial-automation-systems/); [GT Engineering](https://www.gt-engineering.it/en/insights/ul-and-csa-conformity/standard-tecnici-nord-america/)
- FANUC R-30iB controllers can be specified for 380–415V, 440–500V or 500–575V at 50/60Hz, so some EU controllers may accept 480V/60Hz directly; R-30iA/R-30iB Cabinet A transformers have 200–230V and 380–400V taps (third-party, verify with FANUC) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-r-30ib-600v-to-380v-auto-transformer-configuration-guide); [Robots.com R-30iA](https://www.robots.com/fanuc/controller/r-30ia)
- Practical workaround: step-up/down transformer sized ~2x robot nameplate VA (non-OEM guidance) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-s-420f-480v-robot-on-208v-power-step-up-transformer-solution)

**Software licences / warranty / support**
- FANUC software options tied to controller serial; transfers require FANUC involvement and may carry fees; without registered licence formal FANUC support may be unavailable (third-party blog, unverified against FANUC policy) — [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-r30ia-robot-software-options-licensing-and-conversion-guide)
- ABB: moving IRC5 options to another robot requires a new RobotWare licence from local ABB; S4C+ options tied to controller identity; ABB EULA (Automation Builder) bars transfer to another end user without written approval — [ABB tech community](https://tech-community.robotics.abb.com/t/moving-irc5-controller-option/8743); [ABB EULA](https://library.e.abb.com/public/1ead4b7cf1de41c6a8bf7b9ae6b850c3/End%20User%20License%20Agreement_final.pdf); [Industrial Monitor Direct ABB](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/resolving-abb-s4c-m2000a-option-key-transfer-between-robots)

### Inferences
- Landed cost add-on rough model for one large robot + controller (inference, verify with forwarder/broker): crating + EU inland + ocean (LCL or shared/whole 20ft) + US port/customs/inland ≈ $3,000–$8,000; duty ≈ 2.5%–15% of customs value depending on which EU rate is current; HMF 0.125%; MPF (not researched); customs bond. On a €15k robot that is roughly a 25–60% uplift before any electrical work.
- Price US quotes DDP or clearly state Incoterms; most small US buyers will not want to be importer of record.
- Pre-sale checklist per unit: controller voltage options and nameplate, software option list/backup, controller serial for OEM licence check, mastering/calibration data, hours, test video, CE-to-NFPA 79 gap list, buyer's AHJ field-evaluation requirement.
- RIA R15.06 (ANSI/A3 R15.06-2025, harmonized with ISO 10218) governs robot system/cell safety; responsibility sits with integrator/user, so the agent should route buyers through a US integrator for cell safety. (Standard edition/date not verified in this session.)

- Could not fetch Federal Register/CBP texts; current EU all-in rate (10% vs 15%) and robots' status under the Oct 28, 2026 Section 232 proclamation must be confirmed with a licensed customs broker.
- No Bilbao→US robot-specific freight quote or transit time found (typical transatlantic ocean transit ~2–3 weeks port-to-port is general knowledge, unverified here).
- Cost of NRTL field evaluation not found.

## 4. Channels to reach US buyers

### Takeaway
The highest-leverage channels are (1) US system integrators who need affordable arms for customer cells, (2) online listings on the marketplaces US buyers already search (eBay, Machinio, Surplus Record), and (3) trade shows—Automate 2027 (Las Vegas, May 10–13), FABTECH, PACK EXPO Las Vegas 2027—plus education buyers (community colleges with FANUC certification programmes funded by grants).

- Education: FANUC-certification programmes typically require specific FANUC configurations/software (e.g., HandlingTool, certified carts), so used imported FANUC arms may not qualify; generic training cells (ABB/KUKA/Yaskawa) are a niche.

## 5. Value-added opportunities beyond brokering

### Takeaway
RaaS (Formic, Hirebotics) and integrated cells show US buyers pay for outcomes, not arms; an agent could add value by bundling imported used arms with US integration partners, packaged welding/palletizing/tending cells, financing/rental, or education cells—though each adds risk and capital needs.

### Cited Findings

## 6. Practical issues for an India-based agent selling remotely into the US (added per coordinator)

### Takeaway
Remote selling from India is workable under the 10% referral model: from April 2026 (Finance Act 2026), an Indian commission agent paid by a foreign principal can likely treat its service as a zero-rated export (GST under LUT, no 18% output GST), and FEMA lets proceeds be received through an AD bank within nine months of invoice, with monthly EDF reporting from Oct 2026. The reseller model would make the agent a merchanting-trade intermediary (goods go Spain→US without touching India). That is permitted under the new FEMA 2026 regulations, but it is bank-intensive and carries goods risk, so it is a poor first step. Credibility in the US is the binding constraint. The lean answer is to partner with a US integrator or dealer who owns install, support and warranty, while the agent does lead generation and deal coordination.

### Cited Findings
**GST on commission from a foreign principal**
- The Finance Act 2026 omitted IGST Act section 13(8)(b), which had fixed the place of supply of "intermediary services" at the supplier's location in India and so denied export status. Place of supply now falls under the default s.13(2), the recipient's location, so intermediary services to overseas principals "now qualify as zero-rated supplies subject to other prescribed conditions" — [Rödl & Partner](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/budget-2026-amendment-intermediary-service-stand-now/); [Grant Thornton India](https://www.grantthornton.in/insights/articles/gst-on-intermediary-services/); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/omission-of-igst-section-138b-how-budget-2026-resets-taxation-of-intermediary-services-1442779)
- Effective date conflict: Presidential assent on 30 Mar 2026, or 1 Apr 2026, depending on the source. Check against the Finance Act text and any CBIC notification — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/goodbye-section-13-8-b-igst-act-2017-budget-2026-transforms-intermediary-service-taxation.html); [NJ Jain](https://njjain.com/industry-news/finance-act-2026-intermediary-gst-rule-change-explained/)
- Section 2(13) still defines an intermediary as a broker or agent who arranges or facilitates supplies, so a robot-referral agent is an intermediary — [Rödl](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/budget-2026-amendment-intermediary-service-stand-now/)

**FEMA / RBI: receiving commission into India**
- RBI's Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations 2026 consolidate exports, imports and merchanting trade. They take effect around 1 Oct 2026; one source says 6 Oct — [KS&K](https://ksandk.com/newsletter/rbi-fema-export-import-regulations-2026/); [LiveLaw Biz](https://www.livelawbiz.com/compliance/reserve-bank-of-india-foreign-exchange-management-export-and-import-of-goods-and-services-regulations-520678)
- Realisation period for service exports: nine months from invoice (twelve if invoiced in INR) after a September 2026 amendment. Some earlier-2026 guides cite 15 months, which reportedly never came into operation — [Wissen Solutions](https://wissensolutions.in/insights/export-realisation-period-9-months-fema-2026/); [Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/rbi-circular-on-export-realisation/)
- Service exporters file a monthly Export Declaration Form (EDF) with their AD bank from 1 Oct 2026, due within about 30 days of the end of the invoice month. The eBRC is generated on the DGFT portal and is needed for GST refunds and export benefits — [TaxGuru EDF](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-export-declaration-form-edf-rules-export-services-1-october-2026.html); [Startup Advisory](https://www.startupadvisory.in/blog-export-of-services-reporting-rbi-edf-fema-2026.htm); [GoForFiling](https://goforfiling.com/edf-for-service-exports-rbi-2026/)

**Reseller model = merchanting trade**
- The 2026 FEMA rules cover merchanting trade, where goods are bought abroad and sold abroad without entering India. Reported changes: a six-month foreign-exchange outlay window, extended from four months by an Oct 2025 circular; a relaxed or removed nine-month completion cap, with sources conflicting; commissions and third-party payments allowed if the AD bank is satisfied; and AD banks must keep SOPs and report via PRAVAAH — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/rbi-eases-compliance-extends-time-for-merchanting-trade-125100101416_1.html); [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/merchanting-trade-transaction-guide-2026); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/fema-2026-unraveled-tracing-the-shift-from-legacy-import-export-rules)

**Remote-friendly lean models (from earlier sections)**
- US buyers already compare prices on eBay, Machinio and Surplus Record, and dealers there offer warranty and financing, so listings and remote lead generation can reach buyers without a US office — see section 1 and 4 sources ([eBay Robots Done Right](https://www.ebay.com/str/robotsdoneright?_pgn=3); [Machinio](https://www.machinio.com/abb/irb6640))
- US code acceptance (NFPA 79, NRTL field evaluation, AHJ variation) and OEM licence/support issues need local, hands-on parties — see section 3 sources ([Machine Safety Specialists](https://www.machinesafetyspecialists.com/nrtl-services/))

### Inferences
- **Recommended structure:** take the 10% referral (or a referral plus split) as an Indian proprietorship or LLP. Register for GST and file an LUT so the service is zero-rated, invoice Eurobots in EUR, and receive via SWIFT to an AD bank. File the EDF and obtain the FIRA/eBRC. Income is taxable in India as business income. Under the India–Spain DTAA, Spain should not withhold if the agent has no Spanish permanent establishment, but Eurobots may ask for a tax residency certificate (TRC) and Form 10F equivalents (inference; DTAA not researched).
- **Avoid early on:** acting as importer of record in the US, or doing merchanting trade (the reseller model). That route needs US customs bonds, a duty and liability footprint, product liability insurance and FX/working capital, and Indian merchanting compliance with the AD bank.
- **Credibility without US presence:** partner with one or two US integrators or used-robot dealers (multi-brand, Midwest/Southeast) who inspect, install, run NFPA 79 rework and give the US-side warranty. Split the 10% or let the partner add their markup. Add a US virtual phone number, a business address/registered-agent service, and a professional site with Eurobots authorization letter, test videos and inspection reports. Testimonials and case studies come later. A US LLC (formable remotely, e.g., Delaware/Wyoming) helps with optics and US payments, but it adds US tax filings (Form 5472 for foreign-owned single-member LLCs). It is optional for the referral model (inference; not researched).
- **Time zones:** IST is 9.5–10.5 h ahead of US Eastern and 12.5–13.5 h ahead of Pacific. US mornings fall in Indian evenings or nights, so plan evening shifts plus async email/LinkedIn. Spain (CET) overlaps conveniently with Indian afternoons.
- **Agent agreement must-haves:** defined territory (US) and exclusivity (or not); commission base (ex-works invoice, net of freight and duty); when it is earned (on Eurobots receiving full payment); tail period for repeat orders; non-circumvention; registration of leads in writing; currency and transfer fees; governing law and arbitration (Spain vs neutral seat); no authority to bind Eurobots; no warranty liability on the agent. Ask Eurobots about its existing US agents or channel conflicts.

### Gaps
- Exact GST zero-rating conditions post-13(8)(b) (e.g., the s.2(6) IGST export-of-services tests, including that payment is received in convertible foreign exchange) were not checked against the statute text.
- India–Spain DTAA treatment, Spanish withholding practice, and Indian income-tax treatment (e.g., presumptive taxation eligibility) were not researched.
- No sources were found on US buyer attitudes toward offshore sales agents for capital equipment, or on US LLC requirements for foreign owners. These are inferences only.
