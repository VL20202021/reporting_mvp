## 2. Who buys used industrial robots, and why

### Takeaway
Based on dealer descriptions and trade press, the main buyers are (a) small and mid-size manufacturers automating for the first time, (b) system integrators who use used arms to cut quotes, (c) plants keeping legacy lines running, either buying identical spare robots or harvesting parts, and (d) schools and training centers. Motivations are mainly price (roughly 40–60% below new), fast availability compared with long new-robot lead times, and labor shortages. No rigorous survey of used-robot buyer segments exists.

### Cited Findings
- Integrators use used and refurbished robots to lower the cost to their customer without changing the product they deliver. — [Finding Automation](https://www.findingautomation.com/used-industrial-robots) / dealer summaries (search snippet)
- Customers can buy used robots "about 50% cheaper" than new. Refurbished units are available quickly, which cuts lead times compared with new orders. — [Finding Automation](https://www.findingautomation.com/used-industrial-robots) (dealer claim)
- Small businesses are a key buyer group, e.g., wanting help with strenuous tasks "such as loading 50-pound packages onto pallets". "Many buyers of used bots are first-time buyers", who can get a fully refurbished robot that sold new for $80,000 for "$30,000 or so". Large manufacturers sometimes sell robots after "as little as one-fifth of their life span". — [Kiplinger, "How to Get a Good Deal on a Used Robot"](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/business/t057-c000-s010-for-sale-used-robots.html) (older article, years pre-2026)
- The supply side includes US dealers, brokers and marketplaces that also buy surplus robots, controllers and spare parts, for example HGR Industrial Surplus (paint, pick-and-place and welding robots, controllers), Ballard International (robots, systems, spare parts), Robotbids ("businesses of all sizes", including cobots, AMRs and parts), Universal Robotic Inc., Robots.com/RobotWorx, and Robots Done Right. — [HGR](https://hgrinc.com/category/robots/); [Ballard](https://www.ballardintl.com/); [Robotbids](https://robotbids.com/); [Universal Robotic](https://universalroboticinc.com/we-buy-used-robots/); [Robots.com](https://www.robots.com/sell-your-robots); [Robots Done Right](https://robotsdoneright.com/Industrial-robots.html)
- OEMs also sell certified used robots directly (e.g., KUKA used robots, US site). This means Eurobots competes with OEM refurb programs too. — [KUKA used robots](https://www.kuka.com/en-us/services/kuka-used-robots)
- Education is a distinct segment. Suppliers stock "certified used educational robots", and universities are opening robotics training hubs (e.g., University of Maine B.O.T. Loft) to help companies trial automation. — [Education Robots](https://education-robots.com/); [Six Degrees of Robotics Substack](https://sixdegreesofrobotics.substack.com/p/wiring-the-future-inside-americas)
- Lead times: used dealers claim new robots from many brands have waiting lists of more than a year, while used units ship in weeks. One market report claims used robots cut lead times "from 12 months to under 8 weeks" (no source given). A component-level analysis notes harmonic-drive lead times can stretch from 12 to about 40 weeks when demand peaks. — [used-industrialrobots.com blog](https://www.used-industrialrobots.com/blog/best-second-hand-robots-for-buyers-in-2026/); [Astute Analytica](https://www.astuteanalytica.com/industry-report/used-and-refurbished-robots-market) (unverified)
- Used/refurbished robot market-size estimates vary widely and are unreliable: $1.45B (2025) rising to $3.75B (2033); about $5.2B (2024); $5.74B (2025) rising to $10.5B (2035); $8.27B. Several of these attribute North American growth to reshoring and labor shortages. — [Astute Analytica](https://www.astuteanalytica.com/industry-report/used-and-refurbished-robots-market); [Market Report Analytics](https://www.marketreportanalytics.com/reports/used-and-refurbished-robots-61402); [DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/used-and-refurbished-robots-market-47270); [WiseGuy](https://www.wiseguyreports.com/reports/used-industrial-robot-market) (methods undisclosed, low confidence)

### Inferences
- Buyer typology, ranked by likely volume for a KUKA/ABB/Fanuc arm dealer (inference from dealer positioning, not measured data):
  1. Integrators building cells for price-sensitive end users.
  2. SME manufacturers: metal fab and welding job shops, plastics/injection molding (sprue picking, machine tending), CNC job shops (machine tending), foundries and forging (harsh environments where new robots are "wasted"), food/beverage and consumer goods (end-of-line palletizing), woodworking.
  3. Large plants maintaining legacy lines (identical-model replacement, spares, parts harvesting).
  4. Education and training (community colleges, trade schools, universities, workforce centers).
  5. Research labs, robotics startups and makers (small volume, often older or smaller arms).
- Why used fits SMEs: a fully refurbished arm plus controller costs a fraction of new. Robot hardware is often only about 25–35% of total cell cost, though, so used-robot savings matter most when the buyer or integrator can handle the rest. (The hardware-share figure is a common industry rule of thumb and was not verified in this session.)

### Gaps
- No primary survey data on the shares of used-robot buyers by segment, company size or country.
- No Reddit, r/PLC or Practical Machinist threads were retrieved (network and search limits). Hobbyist and maker demand remains anecdotal.
- RobotWorx/Robots.com customer case studies were not retrieved.

## 3. Typical applications for used robots

### Takeaway
Used-robot demand clusters in mature, well-documented applications: arc (MIG) welding, spot welding (often ex-automotive), palletizing and material handling, machine tending (CNC and injection molding), and education. Dealer price lists suggest that refurbished arc-welding arms are the most common entry point for small US fab shops.

### Cited Findings
- Eurobots markets turnkey solutions for welding, laser cutting, handling and palletizing. — [HowToRobot](https://www.howtorobot.com/robot-suppliers/industrial-machinery-export-bilbao-sl-eurobots)
- US surplus brokers sell paint robots, pick-and-place robots, welding robots and controllers. — [HGR](https://hgrinc.com/category/robots/)
- Refurbished welding-arm prices at US dealers: Fanuc Arc Mate 100iB with R-J3iB controller about $11,950; Arc Mate 120iC with R-30iA about $19,950; Arc Mate 120iB-10L refurbished $18,995; newer 120iD $40,995. A surplus Arc Mate 100iB + R-J3iB + Lincoln Power Wave package listed around $4,589 (as-is). — [Rice Automation](https://riceautomation.com/used-fanuc-welding-robots); [BC Industrial Services](https://www.bcindustrialservices.com/welding-robots); [Surplus Record](https://surplusrecord.com/machinery-equipment/industrial-robots/sub/welding-robots/); [Machinio](https://www.machinio.com/cat/fanuc-welding-robot) (listing prices, point in time)
- Used/refurbished Fanuc prices range from about $5,000 to more than $200,000. Certified units in good condition are often $40,000–$50,000. Prices rise when bundled with fencing, vision or software. — [Standard Bots, Fanuc robot price 2026](https://standardbots.com/articles/fanuc-robot-price) (vendor, and a competitor to used robots)
- Material handling has overtaken welding as the largest application category in North American new-robot orders (H1 2026). — [A3 via BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a22489d6-cead-4b80-bf32-368ef88baa16); [A3 Q2 2026](https://www.automate.org/market-intelligence/insights/robot-orders-increase-in-q2-as-automation-demand-broadens-across-industries)
- Palletizing heavy packages (e.g., 50-lb boxes) is a classic first-time used-robot application for small businesses. — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/business/t057-c000-s010-for-sale-used-robots.html)

### Inferences
- For a European dealer selling into the US, large-payload KUKA/ABB/Fanuc arms for palletizing and handling, plus Fanuc and Motoman arc-welding arms, are the most transferable SKUs. Spot-welding robots from automotive lines are plentiful but serve a smaller buyer pool.
- Painting robots and cleanroom robots are niche in the used market because of process-specific dress packs and certification.

### Gaps
- No quantitative breakdown of used-robot sales by application.

## 4. US demand drivers 2025–2026 (reshoring, labor, tariffs, A3/IFR statistics, SME adoption)

### Takeaway
US robot demand is growing again. IFR counts 2025 US installations at about 38,400 (+12%), now second globally after China. A3 reports North American orders up 24.8% in Q3 2026 (10,989 units, $723.5M), with 28,984 units (about $1.89B) in the first nine months. Demand is broadening beyond automotive OEMs into auto components, semiconductors/electronics, plastics/rubber and food. Labor shortages (about 400–500k unfilled manufacturing jobs) remain a core driver. On tariffs: as of Nov 2026, the Section 232 robotics/machinery action ended in a 180-day negotiation window, not immediate duties. Tariff risk on European-made robots is therefore a live uncertainty, but no new robot-specific 232 duty applies yet.

### Cited Findings
**A3 order statistics (North America)**
- Q3 2026: 10,989 robots ordered, worth $723.5M. That is +24.8% in units and +26.1% in value year on year, the strongest quarter of 2026. Automotive component orders rose 90.4%. Semiconductor/electronics/photonics rose 97.6%. Plastics and rubber rose 67.7%. Food and consumer goods fell 23.7%. Automotive OEM orders returned to growth but were still down 17% year to date. Cobots: 1,831 units ($63.8M), 16.7% of units. YTD 9 months: 28,984 robots, about $1.89B. A3 flags input costs, trade uncertainty and project timing as risks. — [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/north-american-robot-orders-climb-24-8-q3-2026-says-a3/); [A3 press release](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/north-american-robot-orders-increase-24-8-in-third-quarter-of-2026); [Manufacturing Dive](https://www.manufacturingdive.com/news/north-american-robot-orders-grew-nearly-25-percent-q3-2026-a3/873300/); [PlasticsToday](https://www.plasticstoday.com/automation/plastics-and-rubber-robot-orders-surge)
- H1 2026: 17,995 robots, $1.166B. That is +2.0% in units and +6.6% in value compared with H1 2025 (17,635 units). Q1 2026 was 9,055 units/$543M (flat). Q2 2026 was 8,940 units/$622M. Automotive OEM orders were down 25% in H1. Non-automotive accounted for 54.8% of orders. Cobots in H1: 2,774 units ($114M), 15.4% of units. — [A3 Q2 2026](https://www.automate.org/market-intelligence/insights/robot-orders-increase-in-q2-as-automation-demand-broadens-across-industries); [The Robot Report Q1 2026](https://www.therobotreport.com/north-american-robot-orders-remain-flat-at-the-start-of-2026/); [MMH](https://www.mmh.com/article/north-american-robot-orders-q1-2026-a3); [BigGo Finance (A3 chairman quote)](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a22489d6-cead-4b80-bf32-368ef88baa16)
- Q3 + H1 check: 17,995 + 10,989 = 28,984. This matches the reported 9-month YTD total.

**IFR installations (US)**
- 2025 US installations: about 38,400 (+12%), passing Japan (36,200) to become the #2 market after China. Automotive 13,500 (−1%). Metal and machinery 3,000 (−15%). Food and beverage 2,900 (+30%). Warehousing/logistics and medical also grew. The preliminary June 2026 figure was about 38,000 (+11%). — [IFR press release, Sep 24 2026 (USA)](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/EN-2026-SEP-24-IFR_Press_Release_WR-USA.pdf); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/u-s-robotics-industry-saw-double-digit-growth-in-2025-says-ifr/); [IFR: Five million robots](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally)

**Labor shortages**
- About 415,000 to nearly 500,000 US manufacturing jobs are unfilled (2025–early 2026). Deloitte/The Manufacturing Institute project 3.8M workers needed by 2033, with 1.9M of those jobs possibly unfilled. — [Snelling, State of US Manufacturing 2026](https://www.snelling.com/insights/ai-tariffs-and-a-talent-cliff-the-state-of-u-s-manufacturing-in-2026/); [JRG Partners](https://www.jrgpartners.com/manufacturing-labor-leadership-shortage-statistics-2026/); [Assembly Magazine](https://www.assemblymag.com/articles/99810-manufacturers-shift-hiring-priorities-as-labor-shortages-reshape-factory-jobs) (secondary aggregators; the Deloitte figure is widely cited)

**Tariffs / Section 232**
- Commerce opened a Section 232 investigation into robotics and industrial machinery imports. On Oct 28, 2026, the President accepted the national-security finding but chose a 180-day negotiation period covering imports "from any country" instead of immediate duties. Tariffs, quotas, fees or an investment-incentive program are kept in reserve. — [GovConFeed](https://govconfeed.com/article/robotics-industrial-machinery-section-232-proclamation-november-2026); [Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg: "No Section 232 Tariffs on Robotics and Industrial Machinery"](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/october/no-section-232-tariffs-on-robotics-and-industrial-machinery); [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/robots-at-the-border-washington-puts-robotics-and-industrial-machinery-on-the-section-232-clock/); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/trump-administration-initiates-section-232-investigation-robotics-and-industrial); [Logfret](https://logfret.com/logfret-insights/u-s-defers-new-tariffs-on-robotics-and-industrial-machinery-as-trade-talks-begin/)
- Conflicting secondary claims on current rates for EU-origin robots: one says industrial robots (HTS 8479.50.00) pay no 232 duty; another says some fixed industrial machinery pays 15% combined (EU deal rate). Base MFN rates for industrial robots are cited as 0%, 2.5% or about 2.7% by different sources. **Unverified, so check the HTSUS and the Proclamation text.** — [Ginger Control](https://gingercontrol.com/blog/section-232-tariff-rates-products); [Camtomx](https://www.camtomx.com/en/aranceles/robot-industrial); [open-factory.com](https://open-factory.com/articles/tariff-schedule-2026/)
- Additional context: [Covington, current and forthcoming 232 actions (Apr 2026)](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/04/current-and-forthcoming-section-232-actions-by-the-trump-administration); [The Boston Report, Robotics Policy 2026](https://thebostonreport.com/2026/10/11/robotics-policy-2026-section-232-congress-bills-market/)

**SME adoption**
- Vendor-cited (Global Growth Insights): more than 42% of small and midsize manufacturers adopting automation in 2025 chose cobots. RaaS and rental models make adoption accessible for SMEs. A survey cited by a vendor says 98% of manufacturers are exploring AI-driven automation, but only 20% feel prepared to use it at scale. — [ServiceRobotCo](https://servicerobotco.com/blog/the-manufacturing-labor-gap-how-cobots-fill-unfilled-roles) (low confidence)

### Inferences
- The 2026 recovery is led by auto components, electronics/semiconductor and plastics. These are tier-2/3 suppliers and molders, the SME profile that typically buys used robots. Plastics/rubber (+67.7% in Q3) is a good fit for used machine-tending and sprue-picking arms.
- Tariff uncertainty cuts two ways for a Spanish exporter. New European robots (KUKA/ABB, often EU-made) could face future 232 duties, which would make used units already in the US, or cheaper used imports, relatively more attractive. But a used robot imported from Spain would face the same tariff regime as new imports from the EU if duties are imposed. The 180-day window (to about late April 2027) is the key date to watch. Whether used goods are valued at transaction value matters; under standard customs valuation, used goods carry lower declared value, so ad valorem duties cost less in absolute terms. (Inference, not verified.)
- Long new-robot lead times plus labor shortages support the "available now, at half price" pitch to US SMEs.

- Census BTOS or ABS official robot-adoption rates for small US manufacturers were not retrieved.
- No data on the size of the US used-robot import trade or on European dealers' share of it.
- The A3 full-report SME breakdown is not public.

## 5. Company sizes and decision-maker job titles

### Takeaway
No source found quantifies buyer company size or job titles for used robots. Dealer positioning ("businesses of all sizes", "first-time buyers", "small businesses") points mainly to SMEs (roughly 10–500 employees) and integrators. In those firms, the decision-maker is typically the owner/president or plant/operations manager, with manufacturing or automation engineers as technical evaluators.

### Cited Findings
- Eurobots: "integrators, small and medium companies and multinationals". — [HowToRobot](https://www.howtorobot.com/robot-suppliers/industrial-machinery-export-bilbao-sl-eurobots)
- Robotbids: "businesses of all sizes". Sigma Robotics: "large and small companies worldwide". — [Robotbids](https://robotbids.com/); [Sigma Robotics](https://www.sigmarobotics.com/robots)
- "Many buyers of used bots are first-time buyers". — [Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/business/t057-c000-s010-for-sale-used-robots.html)

### Inferences
- Likely buying roles (unverified, based on typical SME capital-equipment buying):
  - Small job shops (<50 employees): owner/president, sometimes with the shop foreman.
  - Mid-size plants (50–500 employees): plant manager or operations director as economic buyer; manufacturing, process or automation engineer as champion and evaluator; purchasing handles terms.
  - Integrators: owner, applications engineer, purchasing.
  - Schools: program director or department chair, often grant-funded.
  - Large plants buying spares: maintenance manager or controls/robotics engineer.
- Persona targeting for US outreach should prioritize owners and plant managers at metal fab, plastics and auto-component SMEs, plus the purchasing and engineering staff at integrators.

### Gaps
- No survey data on job titles or company size for used-robot purchases. A Eurobots CRM export or LinkedIn audience data would close this gap.
