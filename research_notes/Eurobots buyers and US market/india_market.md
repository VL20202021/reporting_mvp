## 1. Demand and buyers: who buys industrial robots in India, and who would buy used ones

### Takeaway
India had a record 9,120 industrial robot installations in 2024 (IFR World Robotics 2025), sixth in the world. Reports say IFR's World Robotics 2026 puts 2025 at about 10,500 units (+15%), with about 27% CAGR from 2020 to 2025. Automotive takes about 45% of installations. That makes auto/two-wheeler tier-1 and tier-2 suppliers, and welding in particular, the main pool of used-robot buyers.

### Cited Findings
- 2024: 9,120 units installed, a record and +7% year on year. India ranks 6th, behind China, Japan, the USA, Korea and Germany. Automotive has a 45% share, and automotive installations rose 15% in 2024. — [IFR press release India, 25 Sep 2025](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/2025-09-25-IFR_press_release_India_in_English.pdf); [Tribune India](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/india-rises-to-sixth-in-global-factory-robot-installations)
- 2025 (World Robotics 2026): "almost 10,500 installations, up 15%", 6th globally, average growth of 27% a year from 2020 to 2025. This comes from a search summary and I did not see the IFR primary document. Mark as **unverified**. — [IFR site (search result)](https://ifr.org/home/typo3/P75); [electronicspecifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=164090)
- Most used-robot sellers on IndiaMART are in Pune (Power Robotics & Automation, Prime Robotics Automation, A1 Robotic & Automation), with more listings in Chennai. Their stock is mostly arc-welding robots such as the FANUC ArcMate 100iB/100iC, which points to fabrication and auto-component welding as the main buyer segment. — [IndiaMART Pune robot listings](https://m.indiamart.com/city/pune/robot-machine.html); [Prime Robotics welding robots](https://m.indiamart.com/prime-robotics-automation/welding-robot.html); [IndiaMART Chennai welding robots](https://m.indiamart.com/city/chennai/welding-robots.html)
- A 2026 Indian pricing guide puts an entry-level robotic welding cell at about ₹25–45 lakh. This is a single-source estimate. — [Industrial Automation India, 2026](https://www.industrialautomationindia.in/research-reports/cost-of-robotic-welding-line-india)

### Inferences
- The best-fit buyers for used European robots are price-sensitive tier-2 and tier-3 auto-component and two-wheeler suppliers and job-shop fabricators. Clusters: Pune/Chakan/Chinchwad, Chennai/Sriperumbudur/Oragadam, Gurugram/Manesar, Aurangabad (Waluj/Shendra), Rajkot and Coimbatore. Main applications: MIG/arc welding, spot welding, machine tending and palletizing.
- Engineering colleges, ITIs, polytechnics and skill centres are a credible niche for low-payload used robots (for example small ABB/FANUC/KUKA arms) as training cells. I found no source quantifying this demand.
- Electronics (Noida, Sriperumbudur) uses mainly new SCARA/6-axis robots supplied with the line, so it is a weaker fit for used units. This is inference.

### Gaps
- No sourced breakdown of Indian installations by sector beyond automotive's 45% share, and no MSME automation-adoption survey data.
- PLI scheme links (auto/ACC, electronics) to robot purchases were not researched for lack of time.

## 2. Government schemes supporting automation purchases

### Takeaway
No central scheme was found that directly subsidises buying robots, used or new. SAMARTH Udyog Bharat 4.0 provides awareness, demonstration and consulting only. CLCSS (capital subsidy for technology upgrades) and a reported ₹2,000 crore MSME tech-upgrade scheme need to be checked.

### Cited Findings
- SAMARTH Udyog Bharat 4.0 is a Ministry of Heavy Industries initiative under the "Enhancement of Competitiveness in Indian Capital Goods Sector" scheme. Its centres "do not provide financial assistance" and focus on training, awareness and consulting. — [Vajiram & Ravi](https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/samarth-udyog-bharat-40-initiative/); [Construction World](https://www.constructionworld.in/policy-updates-and-economic-news/samarth-udyog-bharat-4.0-driving-industry-4.0-adoption-in-india/71276)

An earlier report described a 20% capital subsidy on energy-efficient machinery for MSMEs with turnover under ₹50 crore, but it was still "under discussion". Whether it was enacted, and whether it covers robots or used equipment, is **unconfirmed**. — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/indias-msmes-set-for-a-major-tech-boost-with-new-rs2000cr-scheme/tldr); [Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/nl/stories/newspaper/Mint-Kolkata/INDIA-PLANS-2000CR-MSME-TECHNOLOGY-UPGRADE-SCHEME)

## 3. Import rules, duties and logistics for second-hand robots

### Takeaway
Second-hand capital goods such as industrial robots are generally "Free" to import under FTP 2023 (Para 2.31). No licence is needed beyond restricted items like PCs, laptops, photocopiers and ACs, and as far as I found there is no current age limit. Customs normally expects a Chartered Engineer certificate (age, residual life, value) under CBIC Circular 07/2020. The Hazardous Waste Rules can apply if a robot or its controller is treated as a "used electrical and electronic assembly". Duty is about 7.5% BCD plus 18% IGST (aggregator data, unverified).

### Cited Findings
- FTP 2023 Para 2.31: second-hand capital goods are importable. A MoEFCC NOC is needed where the goods fall under the Hazardous Waste Rules. A Chartered Engineer Certificate is required for valuation under CBIC Circular No. 07/2020 (5 Feb 2020). — [Mundhra Consulting MoEF guide](https://mundhraconsulting.com/?p=2382) (secondary source)
- Restricted second-hand capital goods (needing authorisation) are PCs/laptops, photocopiers/multifunction machines, air conditioners and, in older versions, DG sets. Other second-hand capital goods are free. All second-hand goods that are *not* capital goods are restricted. — [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1019922); [PrintWeek](https://printweek.in/news/restriction-import-secondhand-printing-kit-import-policy-12341)
- Refurbished spares of capital goods are free with a Chartered Engineer certificate showing at least 80% residual life. — [PrintWeek](https://printweek.in/news/restriction-import-secondhand-printing-kit-import-policy-12341); [BDO FTP amendments](https://bdo.in/getmedia/66c12148-82d1-4e1e-a222-cfd7fa2bb792/Foreign-Trade-Policy_Key-Amendments_BDO-in-India_2.pdf.aspx?ext=.pdf)
- Historical: a 10-year age limit applied under older FTPs (2002–07, 2009–14). No source found shows such a limit under FTP 2023. Commentary calls age and residual-life tests "procedural" and notes that Chartered Engineer certification is inconsistent. — [taxtmi](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=5705); [Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/in/column/import-of-second-hand-capital-goods:-legal-regime-and-judicial-trends-in-india/view)
- Customs may ask for the Chartered Engineer certificate, ideally from the country of supply, to assess age, condition and value. Valuation falls back to Customs Valuation Rule 9 when there is no transaction value. A tribunal held that the certificate must be accepted or rejected as a whole. — [taxtmi](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=14575); [STPI CE certificate format](https://thiruvananthapuram.stpi.in/sites/default/files/download-format-document/14-F-09-CE%20Certificate%20for%20Import%20of%20Second%20Hand%20Equipment.pdf)
- Pre-shipment inspection agencies are listed in Appendix 2G under HBP 2023 Para 2.52(c). This mainly concerns metal scrap. I found no source requiring a pre-shipment inspection certificate for second-hand robots. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/?p=472906); [EEPC notices](https://www.eepcindia.org/backend/public-notices)
- Hazardous & Other Wastes Rules 2016: under Schedule III Part B entry B1110, "used electrical and electronic assemblies" for direct reuse need MoEFCC permission, which is given within 60 days of State Pollution Control Board approval. EPR authorisation under the E-Waste Rules 2022 may also apply. Imports for repair and re-export, or rental re-exported within a year, are exempt (Schedule III Part D). — [MoEFCC revised checklist](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/2024/05/OM-Revised-Checklist-for-import-export-of-hazardous-and-other-wastes.pdf); [Corpseed](https://corpseed.com/knowledge-centre/norms-around-import-and-export-of-hazardous-wastes-in-india)
- The MoEFCC Expert Committee has approved used-machinery imports case by case, for example for an EMS manufacturer in March 2021 with a residual-life certificate. — [MoEFCC EC 102nd meeting decisions](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/2017/08/Decision-of-the-102nd-meeting-of-Expert-Committee-held-on-17th-March-2021-3.pdf)
- Duty on HS 8479.50 (84795000, industrial robots): 7.5% BCD and 18% IGST. Total landed duty is about 27.7–28.5% depending on whether the social welfare surcharge or AIDC is included. Aggregators only, and one source lists GST as "5% or 18%". **Verify on ICEGATE and the CBIC tariff.** — [treayo](https://treayo.com/en/hs/india/84795000); [eximpe](https://eximpe.com/hsncode-finder/84795000); [patronaccounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/hsn-code/84795000)
- Freight: the only 2026 carrier data found is for the **opposite direction** (MSC, Nhava Sheva to Valencia, 20ft). Rates were USD 1,650 in Jan 2026, 1,950 in Feb, 4,250 in June and 6,250 in July 2026, plus surcharges and origin THC of ₹10,700 per 20ft. There is no Spain-to-India rate or transit time. — [MSC advisory Feb 2026](https://www.msc.com/en/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/february/price-announcement-india-pakistan-to-europe); [MSC June 2026](https://www.msc.com/es/newsroom/customer-advisories/2026/june/price-announcement-trade-from-india-and-pakistan-to-europe)

### Inferences
- In practice: get a Chartered Engineer certificate (ideally from Spain) stating year of manufacture, residual life and fair value. Classify under 8479.50. Budget about 28% duties, of which 18% IGST is creditable for a GST-registered buyer, so the real cost is about 10%. Confirm with a customs broker whether customs at the port treats the controller as falling under B1110 (EEE). Most used machinery clears as a capital good, but there is risk at the port.
- European robots run on 400V/50Hz three-phase, which is compatible with India's 415V/50Hz grid. No compulsory BIS registration was found for industrial robots. Both points are inference, not verified.
- A Spain-to-India 20ft container (the westbound direction from Europe) is normally cheaper than the Asia-to-Europe headhaul. A robot arm plus controller on a pallet usually goes as LCL. Typical sea transit Spain to Nhava Sheva is about 20–30 days (inference, unverified).

### Gaps
- I could not read the official HBP 2023 or FTP 2023 text, or confirm the current Para 2.31 wording.
- No verified Spain-to-India freight rate or transit time, and no customs broker clearance costs.
- AIDC applicability on 8479.50 is unverified.

## 5. Channels, price sensitivity, after-sales, and business and legal set-up

### Takeaway
IndiaMART and TradeIndia are where Indian used-robot buyers search; the competitors listed above all trade there. Commission income from a foreign principal (Eurobots) became export of services from 30 Mar 2026. The Finance Act 2026 omitted IGST Section 13(8)(b), so the fee can be zero-rated under LUT if the FEMA and GST export conditions are met. Acting as importer instead needs an IEC and GST registration and puts duty and working capital on the agent.

### Cited Findings
- IGST Section 13(8)(b), which placed intermediary services "in India" and blocked export status for commission agents, was omitted by Section 157 of the Finance Act 2026, effective 30 March 2026 (date of Presidential assent). The GST Council recommended this at its 56th meeting on 3 Sep 2025. The definition of "intermediary" in Section 2(13) is unchanged, so place of supply now follows the recipient-location rule in Section 13(2). — [Grant Thornton Bharat](https://www.grantthornton.in/insights/articles/gst-on-intermediary-services/); [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/top-stories/omission-of-igst-section-138b-how-budget-2026-resets-taxation-of-intermediary-services-1442779); [Winvesta](https://www.winvesta.in/blog/businesses/budget-2026-killed-section-138b-gst-on-exports)
- Before this change, Indian commission agents for foreign goods sellers were treated as intermediaries, with 18% GST payable and no export benefit; advance rulings held this. — [Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/treatment-for-commission-agents-working-with-foreign-principals/); [Gujarat AAR 41/2020](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/AAR/guj_aar_41_2020_da_30.07.2020.pdf)
- IndiaMART and TradeIndia carry most used-robot sellers (see section 4 listings), and a "Eurobots.Co.In" profile already exists on TradeIndia. — [TradeIndia](https://www.tradeindia.com/eurobots-co-in-5596933/)

### Inferences
- **Agent model:** the agent earns commission from Eurobots in foreign currency. The buyer imports directly, needing its own IEC and paying duty and IGST (input credit available). The agent registers for GST once turnover exceeds ₹20 lakh for services (₹10 lakh in special-category states), or voluntarily to file an LUT. After March 2026, export-of-services treatment should be available if payment arrives in convertible foreign exchange and the other Section 2(6) IGST conditions are met. Income tax applies as business income. FEMA: receipts must come through banking channels against a FIRA or e-BRC. This is low-capital and low-risk. Confirm with a CA.
- **Importer/reseller model:** needs IEC (from DGFT, issued against PAN), GST registration, a customs broker, a Chartered Engineer certificate and working capital for about 28% duty plus freight. It also carries the warranty and service liability. It allows higher margins and local invoicing in INR, which MSMEs and colleges prefer, and GeM sales require an Indian GST-registered seller.
- Channel priorities (inference): IndiaMART paid listing; LinkedIn outreach to plant heads and maintenance managers in Pune, Chennai, NCR and Aurangabad clusters; IMTEX (Bengaluru, January), Automation Expo (Mumbai) and Weld India; ACMA and CII cluster events; partnerships with system integrators who need low-cost arms; and GeM for ITIs and polytechnics, which needs an Indian seller entity.
- Indian buyers are highly price-sensitive. The main objections are service, spares and programming support, so a local service partner and a spares stock (teach pendants, servo amplifiers, cables) are key differentiators. This is inference, unsourced.

### Gaps
- No sourced data on trade-show dates for 2027, association membership costs, or GeM category rules for used equipment (GeM generally favours new goods; unverified).
- No survey data on Indian buyer price sensitivity or financing (NBFC or equipment-loan) practices for used robots.
- FEMA specifics for an individual (as opposed to a company) receiving commission were not verified with a primary RBI source.
