# Take the 10% referral, sell through integrators

**Eurobots (Industrial Machinery Export Bilbao SL) is a real, small, long-established Spanish dealer and refurbisher of used industrial robots.** It has been registered since 2003 (CIF B95266664), has about 20 staff and roughly €2.5–6M in revenue, and holds its own stock in a warehouse near Bilbao airport. It has no US representative. In India it already backs a partner, Catalyst Robotics & Automation in Pune. For an India-based founder, the stronger opportunity is the **US**. A **10% referral commission (option 2)** on a typical €15k–€40k robot or cell earns about €1,500–€4,000 per deal with no inventory, import, tariff or warranty risk. The reseller markup (option 1) would make you a merchanting-trade intermediary, importer and warranty-holder in a market whose electrical code (NFPA 79) and buyer expectations need hands-on local partners. The best US buyers are multi-brand system integrators and small-to-mid manufacturers in metal fabrication, auto components and plastics, where the economic buyer is the owner or plant manager. Integrators repeat-buy, can absorb compliance work, and give you the US credibility you lack. India is a weaker second market: local refurbishers price arms at ₹6–12 lakh, about 28% import duty squeezes margins, and Catalyst makes channel conflict close to certain unless Eurobots gives you written territory and lead protection. **Caveat on sources:** eurobots.net, eurobots.co.in, robot-forum.com, automate.org, ifr.org and several legal and trade sites could not be opened directly during research. Company details, the only buyer review and several tariff and tax points rely on search-engine snippets and summaries. Verify them before you sign or quote a customer.

## Eurobots' buyers are European integrators and SMEs; the US is almost greenfield

Eurobots describes its customers only generically, as "integrators, small and medium companies and multinationals around the globe" ([HowToRobot](https://www.howtorobot.com/robot-suppliers/industrial-machinery-export-bilbao-sl-eurobots)). It publishes **no named customers, no case studies and no US testimonials**. The homepage section "What clients say about us" could not be retrieved. Its languages, trade fairs and the one UK review point to a core buyer base in Spain, Italy, Germany, the UK, Portugal and Latin America. Its emphasis on welding, laser cutting, handling and palletizing points to metal fabricators, automotive suppliers and packaging/logistics users. Any US sales so far look opportunistic. As a result, you would be building the US channel rather than inheriting one, and Eurobots has no existing US representative to conflict with you.

Across the wider used-robot market, buyers fall into five groups, roughly in order of likely volume:

1. **System integrators**, who use refurbished arms to cut cell quotes without changing what they deliver ([Finding Automation](https://www.findingautomation.com/used-industrial-robots)).
2. **SME manufacturers automating for the first time.** Kiplinger notes that "many buyers of used bots are first-time buyers", paying about $30,000 for a robot that cost $80,000 new ([Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/article/business/t057-c000-s010-for-sale-used-robots.html)).
3. **Large plants keeping legacy lines running** with identical spares.
4. **Education and training buyers.**
5. **Labs and startups.**

The motives are price (roughly 40–60% below new), availability in weeks rather than months, and labor shortages. Demand concentrates in mature applications: arc and spot welding, palletizing and handling, and CNC and injection-molding machine tending.

## US demand is rising, but the competition is mature and price-transparent

**The demand backdrop favors you.** IFR counts **about 38,400 US robot installations in 2025 (+12%)**, which makes the US the world's second-largest market ([IFR](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/EN-2026-SEP-24-IFR_Press_Release_WR-USA.pdf)). A3 reports North American orders **up 24.8% in Q3 2026 to 10,989 units ($723.5M)**. The growth is led by automotive components (+90.4%), semiconductors and electronics (+97.6%) and **plastics and rubber (+67.7%)** ([A3](https://www.automate.org/robotics/news/north-american-robot-orders-increase-24-8-in-third-quarter-of-2026); [The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/north-american-robot-orders-climb-24-8-q3-2026-says-a3/)). These are tier-2/3 suppliers and molders, the profile that buys used arms. About 415,000–500,000 US manufacturing jobs are unfilled ([Snelling](https://www.snelling.com/insights/ai-tariffs-and-a-talent-cliff-the-state-of-u-s-manufacturing-in-2026/)), which keeps the "available now, half price" pitch relevant.

## Import friction lives in electrical code and licences, more than tariffs

**Tariffs are uncertain and the sources conflict.** Industrial robots fall under **HTS 8479.50.00, with a 2.5% MFN duty** ([HTSHub](https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/detail/8479500000)). On top of that, EU goods pay a Section 301 surcharge that replaced the expired Section 122 surcharge on July 24, 2026 ([Torres Trade Law](https://www.torrestradelaw.com/posts/Global-Tariffs,-Repackaged:-USTR-Announces-New-Section-301-Tariffs-on-a-Broad-Range-of-Countries/448)). Sources disagree on whether the EU all-in rate is **10%** ([ustariffrates.com](https://ustariffrates.com/tariff-rates/eu)) or **15%** ([tariffstool.com](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/section-122-tariff-rates-2026)). On **October 28, 2026**, the President accepted the Section 232 national-security finding on robotics and industrial machinery but **imposed no duties yet**. He opened a 180-day negotiation window that runs to about **late April 2027**, with tariffs or quotas held in reserve ([Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg](https://www.strtrade.com/trade-news-resources/str-trade-report/trade-report/october/no-section-232-tariffs-on-robotics-and-industrial-machinery); [Troutman Pepper Locke](https://www.troutman.com/insights/robots-at-the-border-washington-puts-robotics-and-industrial-machinery-on-the-section-232-clock/)). Add the 0.125% harbor maintenance fee and about $150–$300 for customs entry ([Freight-Calculator](https://blog.freight-calculator.com/20-ft-container-shipping-cost-2026)). A licensed US customs broker must confirm the actual rate before you quote anyone. A 20ft container runs roughly $1,200–$2,800 in base ocean freight, plus $800–$2,500+ in port, documentation and inland charges, and open-top or oversize units cost more ([Freight-Calculator](https://blog.freight-calculator.com/20-ft-container-shipping-rates)).

**The harder problems are compliance and licences.** A machine built to the European standard EN 60204-1 "could not be installed in North America" as-is under NFPA 79 ([Certifico](https://certifico.com/component/attachments/download/2338)). The **CE mark is not accepted in place of an NRTL listing**, so imported controllers may need a field evaluation, with requirements set by each local authority ([Machine Safety Specialists](https://www.machinesafetyspecialists.com/nrtl-services/); [IAEI](https://iaeimagazine.org/2004/november2004/certification-of-factory-automation-equipment/)). Some FANUC R-30iB controllers accept 440–500V at 60Hz, so certain EU units can run on US 480V. Others need a transformer ([Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-r-30ib-600v-to-380v-auto-transformer-configuration-guide)). Software options are tied to the controller. ABB's licence terms bar transfer to a new end user without written approval, and FANUC options are tied to the controller serial, so moving them may require OEM involvement and fees ([ABB EULA](https://library.e.abb.com/public/1ead4b7cf1de41c6a8bf7b9ae6b850c3/End%20User%20License%20Agreement_final.pdf); [Industrial Monitor Direct](https://industrialmonitordirect.com/blogs/knowledgebase/fanuc-r30ia-robot-software-options-licensing-and-conversion-guide)). Each of these issues needs someone on site. That is why a US integrator partner is required rather than optional, and why the reseller option would put risks on you that you cannot manage from India.

## India is a crowded, low-ticket market where Eurobots already has a partner

**Demand exists and is concentrated in automotive.** India installed a record **9,120 industrial robots in 2024**, ranking sixth globally, with automotive at **45%** of installations ([IFR India](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/2025-09-25-IFR_press_release_India_in_English.pdf)). Reported 2025 figures of about 10,500 units (+15%) come from search summaries and are unverified ([electronicspecifier](https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=164090)). The best-fit buyers are tier-2/3 auto-component and two-wheeler suppliers and job-shop fabricators in the Pune/Chakan, Chennai/Sriperumbudur, Gurugram/Manesar, Aurangabad, Rajkot and Coimbatore clusters. Typical personas are the plant head, maintenance manager and owner. Engineering colleges and ITIs form a training niche. Selling to them through GeM requires an Indian GST-registered seller.

**Local prices set a low ceiling.** Pune and Chennai refurbishers list used FANUC arc-welding arms at **₹6.5–12 lakh** (about $7k–$14k) and complete welding systems at ₹21–25.5 lakh ([IndiaMART Prime Robotics](https://m.indiamart.com/prime-robotics-automation/welding-robot.html); [IndiaMART Chennai](https://m.indiamart.com/city/chennai/welding-robots.html)). European dealers already sell directly to Indian buyers on Machineseeker-India with 1-year warranties ([Machineseeker-India](https://www.machineseeker-india.com/mss/fanuc+robot?page=7)), which beats Eurobots' 6 months. Imports of second-hand capital goods are generally "Free" under FTP 2023 and need a Chartered Engineer certificate covering age, residual life and value ([Mundhra Consulting](https://mundhraconsulting.com/?p=2382)). Controllers can occasionally trigger used-electronics (Hazardous Waste Rules B1110) scrutiny ([MoEFCC checklist](https://moef.gov.in/uploads/2024/05/OM-Revised-Checklist-for-import-export-of-hazardous-and-other-wastes.pdf)). Duty on 8479.50 is about **7.5% BCD plus 18% IGST**, an aggregator figure to verify on ICEGATE ([treayo](https://treayo.com/en/hs/india/84795000)). The IGST is creditable for GST-registered buyers. Taken together, a Eurobots arm has to leave Bilbao at roughly **€4–8k** to compete on common welding models. **A 10% commission on that is only €400–800 per deal.**

**Catalyst Robotics is the deciding factor.** eurobots.co.in states: "Our partner in India Catalyst Robotics & Automation (Chinchwad Pune)." Catalyst buys, sells and refurbishes used robots itself ([eurobots.co.in](https://www.eurobots.co.in/)). It is a 2007-incorporated private company (CIN U29299PN2007PTC130583) with reported FY2025 revenue of about **₹41 crore**, an aggregator figure not checked against MCA filings ([TheCompanyCheck](https://www.thecompanycheck.com/company/catalyst-robotics-and-automation-private-limited/U29299PN2007PTC130583)). Indian inquiries through the Eurobots site almost certainly go to Catalyst or to Bilbao. A Pune-area TradeIndia listing called "Eurobots.Co.In" also exists, and its link to either company is unclear ([TradeIndia](https://www.tradeindia.com/eurobots-co-in-5596933/)). If you sell in India without a written carve-out, you will compete with an incumbent that Eurobots backs, has local refurbishing and service, and may hold exclusivity. That creates real risk that Eurobots credits your leads to Catalyst or bypasses you. Three viable India paths exist: a defined territory or segment away from Pune (for example South India, NCR, or education/GeM), a sub-agent arrangement with Catalyst itself, or sourcing from other European dealers instead.

## Option 2 wins: low risk, fair rate, and a clean India tax path

**The 10% rate is fair.** Machinery brokers typically charge **5–15%** of sale price ([Ironmart](https://www.ironmartonline.com/standard-commission-rates-for-machinery-brokers-in-2026/)), with 6–12% the common national range ([Grizz Equip](https://www.grizzequip.com/how-much-do-equipment-brokers-charge/)). A published finder's-fee contract uses exactly **10% of net value** ([idrywood](https://idrywood.com/used-equipment-broker-terms)). For a pure introduction with no inventory or warranty exposure, 10% is market-standard to generous.

**Option 1 adds risks you cannot yet carry.** Under the reseller model you would buy at Eurobots' EXW quote and resell to a US buyer, with goods moving Spain to US without touching India. Under RBI's 2026 FEMA export-import regulations that is **merchanting trade**. It is permitted but bank-intensive: your AD bank must apply its procedures and file PRAVAAH reporting within a six-month outlay window ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/economy/news/rbi-eases-compliance-extends-time-for-merchanting-trade-125100101416_1.html); [EximPe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/merchanting-trade-transaction-guide-2026)). On top of that you would carry the 30% deposit and 70% pre-shipment payment, EUR/USD exposure, freight damage, the US duty uncertainty above, NFPA 79 rework and US-side warranty expectations, all against US dealers who offer local warranty and financing. An inferred 20–35% markup would be needed just to cover landed costs. In India, a reseller would need an IEC, GST registration, a customs broker and working capital for about 28% duty. The real argument for option 1 is that it allows bundled cells and INR invoicing. It belongs in a later phase, ideally through a partner who holds the inventory.

**The India tax basics for option 2 changed in your favor in 2026.** Until 2026, Indian commission agents for foreign principals were "intermediaries" whose place of supply was India, so they paid **18% GST** with no export benefit ([Tally](https://tallysolutions.com/gst/treatment-for-commission-agents-working-with-foreign-principals/)). The **Finance Act 2026 omitted IGST Act section 13(8)(b)**. The place of supply now follows the recipient's location, so commission paid by Eurobots in Spain can be a **zero-rated export of services** if you file an LUT and meet the section 2(6) conditions, including receipt in convertible foreign exchange ([Grant Thornton Bharat](https://www.grantthornton.in/insights/articles/gst-on-intermediary-services/); [Rödl & Partner](https://www.roedl.com/en/insights/budget-2026-amendment-intermediary-service-stand-now/)). Sources differ on the effective date (30 March vs 1 April 2026) ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/goodbye-section-13-8-b-igst-act-2017-budget-2026-transforms-intermediary-service-taxation.html)). Under FEMA, service-export proceeds must arrive through an AD bank within **nine months of invoice**. From October 2026 you also file a **monthly Export Declaration Form** with the bank and generate an eBRC on the DGFT portal ([Wissen Solutions](https://wissensolutions.in/insights/export-realisation-period-9-months-fema-2026/); [TaxGuru EDF](https://taxguru.in/rbi/rbi-export-declaration-form-edf-rules-export-services-1-october-2026.html)). The commission is ordinary business income for Indian income tax. A practical setup is a proprietorship or LLP with GST registration, an LUT, EUR invoices to Eurobots, and SWIFT receipt into an AD bank account. India–Spain treaty withholding was not researched; ask Eurobots whether it wants a tax residency certificate. Confirm all of this with a chartered accountant before the first invoice.

**Settle these points with Eurobots before you agree.**

## Conclusion

The offer's value lies less in the 10% rate than in the **gap Eurobots has left open**. It ships to North America but has no US representative, no USD pricing and no US references, while US robot orders are growing at their fastest rate of 2026 in exactly the SME segments that buy used equipment. The bottleneck is **trust and local execution**, not demand. US buyers can already buy a warrantied FANUC welder from an Ohio dealer for $19,000. So you should position yourself as the coordinator between Eurobots' inventory and a US integrator's installation and warranty, and lead with scarce brands, late-generation controllers and complete cells rather than commodity arms.

India looks like the natural home market but is the weaker bet for this deal. Tickets are small, local refurbishers are entrenched, and Eurobots already has a partner there. Two dates matter. The Section 232 window closing around April 2027 could make imported robots more expensive overnight, or make used units already in the US more valuable. Automate 2027 in May is the natural point to scale up if the first 30 days produce quotes. Several findings depend on pages that could not be opened, including Eurobots' own site, the forum review and some tariff and tax texts. Treat this report as a basis for negotiation, and verify the details before you commit.
