# Win robotics customers through paid, gated pilots

An early-stage company that sells AI software together with robotics hardware should not run four city launches at once. It should sell one narrow, measurable job, done indoors and on private property, to **mid-market operations buyers in warehousing/3PL and general-industry material handling**. The first deal should be a **paid, 8–12 week pilot with written KPIs and a pre-agreed conversion price**, and the company should feed **structured public and corporate pilot programmes** in parallel. Every robotics first-customer story in the research notes started with a narrow vertical wedge and a single anchor site: Gecko in boilers, Formic in SMB palletising, Agility in tote moving at GXO, Figure at one BMW plant, Exotec at Cdiscount, Wandercraft at Renault Douai. None started with a horizontal platform sale. On the evidence, **Paris is the best first city**, provided the team can work in French. It has the largest non-dilutive funding stack, French public buyers can award an innovative purchase **below €100,000 excl. VAT directly, without a tender**, and a French reference carries across the EU single market. **New York/New Jersey is the second city.** It has the highest deal sizes and the most portable logo, but its documented robotics path took about two years and carries union and public-safety political risk. **Dubai** is a cheap, programme-driven parallel track that is well timed: GITEX runs 7–11 December 2026. Its programmes have historically produced MoUs more than paid contracts. **Mumbai** should come last unless the founders are Indian. Its pilot tickets are small, and at Maharashtra wages a single-shift labour-replacement ROI does not close. All of this rests on thin evidence. The notes found **no robotics-specific pilot-to-paid conversion rates, no disclosed first-deal values for the famous anchor deals, and no verified French or Emirati pilot price benchmarks**, and several primary sources (First Round Review, IFR, EY, La French Tech) were blocked. Treat the numbers below as planning ranges, not benchmarks.

> **How to read this report.** Claims are tagged **[Fact]** (sourced), **[Opinion]** (expert, practitioner or vendor view; vendor statistics are flagged), or **[Inference]** (my synthesis from the notes, not directly sourced). Where the research notes flagged a figure as unverified or from a blocked source, that caveat is kept.

> **Assumptions, because stage and team are unknown.** This report assumes a pre-seed or seed company of 3–10 people, US$1–5M raised, and a working prototype at roughly TRL 5–6 that can safely run 1–3 units at a customer site with remote or human-in-the-loop support. It assumes no legal entity yet in France, the UAE or India, and founders who can relocate for at least one quarter. It also assumes the product is an indoor mobile or manipulation system (AMR, picking or handling cell, or inspection robot) with an AI layer that can be sold separately. If the company instead makes sub-US$1k developer hardware, or software only, the ranking changes. The developer/PLG and cloud-marketplace motions then move up, and the recommendation flips toward NYC.
>
> **FX assumptions:** AED 3.6725 = US$1 is the official peg ([Fact], per the Dubai notes). ₹88 ≈ US$1 is the research notes' own planning assumption. €1 ≈ US$1.15 is my planning assumption and is not sourced. Re-check all three before quoting prices.

## 1. Executive summary: three strategies, one segment, one first city

**The top three strategies, in order of use:**

1. **Founder-led selling into one vertical wedge, converted into a paid, KPI-gated pilot priced as Robots-as-a-Service (RaaS).** This is the only motion with consistent evidence across hardware and AI. Robotics investors advise startups to deploy the first units themselves and add integrators only at tens to hundreds of units ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/robotics_invest_panelists_discuss_startup_strategy_vc_criteria_in_boston/autonomy)) [Opinion]. Sierra required its design partners to pay and reportedly converted all of them ([First Round Review, snippet only](https://review.firstround.com/sierra-design-partnership.md)) [Opinion; full source blocked]. RaaS removes the buyer's capex hurdle: Formic bills from about **US$8/hour** and only once the system is running ([Robotics & Automation News](https://roboticsandautomationnews.com/2022/12/08/formic-makes-robotics-available-to-hire-by-the-hour/58546)) [Fact].
2. **Structured public and corporate pilot programmes as the fastest route to a paying logo.** These include Transit Tech Lab (eight-week PoCs with MTA, PANYNJ and NYC DOT) ([Cities Today](https://cities-today.com/?p=72068)), French "achat innovant" direct award under €100k excl. VAT ([collectivites-locales.gouv.fr](https://www.collectivites-locales.gouv.fr/index.php/animer-les-territoires/commande-publique/les-seuils-et-taux-applicables-aux-contrats-de-la-commande-publique)), Dubai Future Accelerators ([DFF](https://www.dubaifuture.ae/dfa-news/30-companies-16-countries-taking-part-sixth-cohort-dubai-future-accelerators-program/)), Maharashtra pilot work orders up to **₹25 lakh** ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/maharashtra-cabinet-unveils-startup-policy-to-nurture-entrepreneurs/2714236/)), and venture-client units such as BMW Startup Garage, which buys a "first unit" and issues a supplier number and PO ([BMW](https://www.bmwstartupgarage.com/en/faq.html)) [Fact]. Their weakness is conversion: the NYPD's K5 pilot ended with no follow-on ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/nyc-ends-trial-run-of-times-square-subways-security-robot-105528275.html)), and Dubai's accelerator produced **MoUs, not purchase contracts** ([Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/en-ae/entrepreneurs/dubai-future-accelerators-invests-in-19-projects-graduating/286821)) [Fact].
3. **A strategic investor who is also the customer, then channel through the incumbent service contractor or integrator to go from 1 to 10.** Durst Ventures invested in Skyline Robotics, and Skyline deployed at a Durst tower through a window-cleaning contractor ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/08/28/skylines-robotic-window-washer-debuts-at-durst-office-tower)). Renault took a stake in Wandercraft and deployed its Calvin robot at Douai ([FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/renault-monte-au-capital-de-wandercraft-et-mise-sur-la-robotique-medicale-pour-reinventer-ses-usines/455216)). Reliance bought 55% of Addverb and automated its own grocery warehouse ([Addverb](https://addverb.com/warehouse-automation-case-studies/reliance-automated-grocery-warehouse/)) [Fact].

**Recommended starting segment and industry.** Start with mid-market operations buyers: 3PLs, distributors and general-industry manufacturers with 200–2,000 staff and 1–10 sites, running 2–3 shifts on a repetitive material-handling or picking task. **[Inference]** Transport and logistics took **52%** of the roughly 200,000 professional service robots sold in 2024 ([IFR](https://ifr.org/home/P25)) [Fact]. Non-automotive general industry outpaced automotive in North American robot orders in 2025 ([A3](https://www.automate.org/robotics/industry-statistics/robot-orders-grow-6-6-in-2025-as-general-industries-drive-broader-automation-adoption)) [Fact]. Mid-market buyers close in months rather than the **6–12+ months** an enterprise robotics deal takes ([VentureFizz job posting](https://venturefizz.com/job/tutor-intelligence-enterprise-account-executive-robotics-warehouse-automation/)) [Fact, single company]. A parallel public track in **infrastructure inspection and maintenance** fits TTL, DEWA, French transport operators and Indian defence challenges.

**Recommended first city: Paris, then NYC/NJ.** [Inference] Paris combines the deepest non-dilutive funding, a no-tender public pilot route, a published list of corporate buyers committed to purchasing from startups (Je choisis la French Tech passed **€2bn** in June 2026 and now extends EU-wide; [La French Tech](https://lafrenchtech.gouv.fr/app/uploads/2026/06/805_-_CP_-_Je_choisis_la_French_Tech_franchit_le_cap_des_2_milliards_deuros_et_setend_a_toute_lEurope.pdf)), and precedents (Exotec–Cdiscount, Wandercraft–Renault) where a French anchor became an export reference. Its weaknesses are late payment (**only 48%** of large French companies paid within the legal maximum in 2024; [Banque de France](https://www.banque-france.fr/fr/communiques-de-presse/le-rapport-annuel-2025-de-lobservatoire-des-delais-de-paiement-des-resultats-contrastes-selon-les)) [Fact] and EU machinery rules that tighten from **20 January 2027**. If the founders are US-based or cannot work in French, reverse the order: start in the NJ logistics corridor, not Manhattan.

### Industries: start with warehousing and general-industry handling

Then come **professional cleaning/facilities** (13% of service-robot units), **hospitality/food service** (23%), and **retail**, which now has a certification route through UL 3300 ([UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/news/ul-solutions-grants-first-global-certification-public-facing-robot-simbes-tally-autonomous)) [Fact]. Market forecasts here are volatile. The city adjustment matters: in NYC proper, commercial real estate and transit inspection are the documented buyers. Warehousing demand sits in New Jersey, a market the notes did not research.

## 4. Each strategy succeeds or fails on its conversion mechanics

### Founder-led selling works when the founder chooses the wedge before the account

**How it works.** The founder builds a list of 30–100 target accounts through network, alumni and industry-sponsored projects, runs discovery calls, and converts a few into paid pilots [Inference]. Race Capital argues the founder must personally own sales for roughly the first 20 customers ([Race Capital](https://medium.com/racecapital/10-lessons-learned-from-founder-led-sales-for-startups-3b558fd06782)) [Opinion]. Gecko Robotics shows the pattern at its most frugal. A coal plant sponsored a college senior-design project. A **US$10K** state grant funded the prototype. The founders returned under an order to inspect the boiler, and the company reached product-market fit only around 2017, with "a handful" of power customers ([Ben Franklin](https://cnp.benfranklin.org/?p=4043); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/04/17/gecko-founding-jake-loosararian-robots-infrastructure-environment-y-combinator)) [Fact]. **Fit:** both AI and robotics. Robotics buyers need on-site trust that only founders can create. **Time to revenue:** 3–6 months to a paid pilot for mid-market [Inference]. **Cost:** mostly founder time and travel inside a 2–4 hour service radius. **Risk:** founders over-customise for a champion who lacks budget authority [Inference]. The outreach maths is unforgiving. Average cold-email reply rates sit around **3.4%**, with the top decile above 10.7% ([Instantly](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-reply-rate-benchmarks)) [Vendor], and published averages range from 0.45% to 3.43% depending on the denominator ([Belkins](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates.md)) [Vendor]. Assume 300 well-targeted contacts yield about 10 replies and 4–6 meetings per city per month [Inference].

### Paid pilots succeed only when conversion is negotiated before the robot ships

**How it works.** The pilot has a written success-criteria sheet, a fixed timeline, a fee credited toward the contract, and a pre-agreed production price and volume. Practitioner guidance puts the pilot fee at **10–30% of first-year ACV, 100% credited on conversion** ([Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/how-to-structure-enterprise-pilot-program-pricing-effective-proof-of-concept-strategies)) [Opinion/Vendor]. Robotics GTM advice says the scope should "name the tasks, the metric to track, who owns the decision, what access you get, and the exact conversion path" ([Financial Models Lab](https://financialmodelslab.com/blogs/how-to-open/robotics-in-warehouses)) [Opinion]. A secondary roundup says Sierra's design partners paid **10–20% of total contract value** over 2–6 months. That figure is **unverified** because the original was blocked ([roundup](https://onepage-research.sliplane.app/articles/review.firstround.com/the-30-best-pieces-of-company-building-advice-we-heard-in-2025)). **Real examples:** Agility's GXO proof of concept became a multi-year RaaS deal in roughly 6–9 months ([GXO](https://investors.gxo.com/news-releases/news-release-details/gxo-signs-industry-first-multi-year-agreement-agility-robotics)). Figure's BMW deal was milestone-gated and began with a single robot ([PR Newswire](https://prnewswire.com/news-releases/figure-announces-commercial-agreement-with-bmw-manufacturing-to-bring-general-purpose-robots-into-automotive-production-302036263.html)). Dexterity's FedEx collaboration needed validation "over several years" before scaling ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/fedex-moves-closer-to-deploying-robots-that-can-load-trailers)) [Fact]. None disclosed financial terms. **Risk:** pilot purgatory (see section 6). Claims that paid pilots convert 3–5x better, or at 68%, are **unverified vendor figures** ([Rework](https://resources.rework.com/libraries/deal-closing/pilot-programs)).

### RaaS gets the first signature but draws on the startup's balance sheet

**How it works.** The buyer pays a monthly or hourly fee covering hardware, software and maintenance, so the purchase is opex and avoids the capex committee. Formic's early customers were small first-time automators such as Compact Industries and Georgia Nut. At Polar, the production manager compared about **US$8/hour** for a robot with a **US$15/hour** minimum wage ([Robotics 24/7](https://robotics247.com/article/formic_offers_robotics_as_a_service_and_financing_to_manufacturers)) [Fact]. Vendor benchmarks put warehouse robots at **US$1,500–4,500 per robot per month** ([MetricRig](https://metricrig.pages.dev/answers/robotics-as-a-service-warehouse-cost-2026)), with Brightpick at US$1,900–2,200 ([Brightpick](https://brightpick.ai/resources/how-brightpicks-raas-works/)) and cleaning or hospitality robots at **US$300–800** ([Grabarobot](https://grabarobot.com/blog/robot-as-a-service-raas-guide/)) [Vendor]. **Risks:** "RaaS is not just a robot sold on subscription" and needs real fleet operations ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/why-raas-needs-more-than-a-subscription-model/)) [Opinion]. Per-task leases "can prove riskier for robotics companies, especially startups" ([Built In](https://builtin.com/robotics/robotics-as-a-service-raas)) [Opinion]. Hardware-enabled SaaS runs at **40–60% gross margin** ([Deploy95](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://deploy95.substack.com/p/the-economics-of-poc)) [Opinion]. **Mitigation** [Inference]: offer RaaS to the buyer but move the asset to a third-party equipment lessor, as Formic does by offering financing alongside RaaS.

### Public pilot programmes give access and a logo, not a guaranteed contract

**How it works.** An agency publishes a challenge, selects a cohort, and runs a short PoC on real infrastructure. **NYC:** TTL 2025 chose 12 companies from 112 for eight-week PoCs. The Inspection & Maintenance track is the best fit for robotics, and NYC startup Kinexio worked with both MTA and PANYNJ ([Railway Age](https://www.railwayage.com/passenger/nyc-transit-tech-lab-announces-2025-project-partners/)) [Fact]. **Paris:** a pilot priced under **€100k excl. VAT** and documented as innovative can be awarded without advertising or competition. The general no-tender threshold for supplies and services rose to **€60k** under Décret 2025-1386 ([AP-HP juridique](https://affairesjuridiques.aphp.fr/textes/decret-n-2025-1386-du-29-decembre-2025-modifiant-certains-seuils-relatifs-aux-marches-publics/)) [Fact]. A proposed rise of the innovative threshold to €140k was **not confirmed**, so check Légifrance. **Dubai:** DFA is a nine-week programme in which entities such as RTA, DEWA, Dubai Police and Dubai Municipality set challenges. Cohort 1 produced 19 pilot MoUs and cohort 2 produced 28 ([Entrepreneur](https://entrepreneur.com/en-ae/entrepreneurs/dubai-future-accelerators-invests-in-19-projects-graduating/286821)) [Fact]. **Mumbai:** Maharashtra Startup Week selectees can receive **₹25 lakh** pilot work orders, and departments earmark 0.5% of their outlay for innovation ([Goodreturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/maharashtra-startup-entrepreneurship-innovation-policy-2025-011-1447633.html)) [Fact]. **Risk:** a pilot is not a customer. The notes found no conversion data for TTL or DFA, and the K5 case ended with nothing.

### Grants fund the proof that wins the customer

Non-dilutive money does not count as a customer, but it pays for the evidence a first customer needs. Gecko's US$10K grant did exactly that, and Shield AI's first major contract was a reported **US$1M, nine-month** DIUx prototype award ([Fortune](https://www.fortune.com/2021/07/27/shield-ai-defense-contractor-drone-eye-on-a-i)) [Fact; sources inconsistent on details]. France stacks deepest (section 5). The EU's EIC Accelerator offers up to **€2.5M grant plus €1–10M equity** ([Hezelburcht](https://www.hezelburcht.com/en/news/eic-launches-2026-work-programme-an-overview-of-the-eic-instruments-for-2026/)) [Fact] and scores traction, so a signed sub-€100k public pilot strengthens an application [Inference]. In India, iDEX ADITI covers up to **50% of development, capped at ₹25 crore** ([iDEX PDF](https://idex.gov.in/uploads/page/aditi_scheeme.pdf)) [Fact] and attaches a named defence end-user. In the US, NSF SBIR Phase I rose to **US$305K**, but authorisation lapsed from late 2025 into 2026 ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/sbir-sttr-reauthorization-lapse-2026-what-small-businesses-should-do)) [Fact, aggregator].

### Venture clients and strategic investors turn a pilot into a supplier relationship

BMW Startup Garage buys a "first unit" instead of equity and issues a supplier number and first PO. BMW reports **20 startups** became regular suppliers ([BMW](https://www.press.bmwgroup.com/global/article/detail/T0426038EN/bmw-startup-garage-rolls-out-innovations-in-record-time)) [Fact, older]. Strategic investment goes further. Wandercraft's Calvin handles tyres at Renault Douai with an engineer supervising ([Génération NT](https://www.generation-nt.com/actualites/renault-robots-calvin-usines-2077073)), and 350 robots are reportedly planned [Fact]. Tata Steel's TomorrowLAB has run **about 40 PoCs and pilots since 2019**, and in August 2025 it launched an accelerator that names robotics and automation as a focus ([Tata Steel](https://www.tatasteel.com/corporate/our-organisation/innovation/tomorrowlab/); [IndianWeb2](https://www.indianweb2.com/2025/08/tata-steel-iit-bhubaneswar-india.html)) [Fact]. **Risk:** open-innovation units are door-openers, not budget holders [Inference]. Strategic equity can also scare off the investor's competitors.

### Channel and incumbent contractors move a company from 1 to 10, rarely to 1

Skyline gave Platinum, a building-maintenance contractor, exclusive New York rights to its window-cleaning robot ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20220927005553/en)). It then deployed at a Durst tower with Palladium, still with a human operator ([BSM](https://bsmmag.com/Articles/2024/Daily-Articles/08/Future-of-Window-Cleaning-Ozmo-Deployed-in-NYC.html)) [Fact]. Brain Corp rode Tennant's installed base, and Tennant later invested **US$32M** ([Automated Warehouse](https://www.automatedwarehouseonline.com/tennant-invests-32m-brain-corp-develop-market-more-floor-cleaning-robots/)) [Fact]. IEEE Spectrum notes that many robotics founders neglect system integrators, even though manufacturers rely on them to select and maintain automation ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/five-dos-and-donts-on-building-your-robotics-startup)) [Opinion]. Integrators therefore matter by customer 5–10 [Inference].

### Services-first and captive customers sidestep the trust problem

Gecko sold inspection outcomes performed by its own robots and crew ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=1295411)) [Fact]. The buyer did not need to trust a robot, only a result. Locus started inside Quiet Logistics, a 3PL that became customer zero after Amazon stopped supporting third-party Kiva users ([FreightWaves](https://www.freightwaves.com/news/quiet-logistics-spinoff-growing-robotics-as-a-service-business-with-video)) [Fact]. Both are strong first-customer routes. Services-first risks turning into an agency, and the captive route is only available if the founders or a parent company already operate a facility [Inference].

### Marketplaces, PLG and events amplify demand but do not create it

AWS Marketplace makes "AWS the seller of record" and lets buyers draw down committed spend ([Marketeering](https://www.marketeering.ai/?p=7872)) [Opinion]. That applies only to the software or fleet layer. The 40%-faster and 80%-larger claims come from studies **AWS commissioned**. PLG works for Hugging Face's open-source LeRobot and the **US$299–500** Reachy Mini ([Hackster](https://hackster.io/news/hugging-face-makes-its-move-in-robotics-67a777220d46)) [Fact], not for US$100K cells. Trade shows such as MODEX, Labelexpo for Formic, GITEX and VivaTech amplify anchor deals rather than close them [Inference].

## 5. Four city playbooks: who buys, who funds, what to file

### New York / New Jersey: sell indoors, through incumbents, with SOC 2 in progress

**Top buyers.** Commercial landlords are the best documented: Durst, via Durst Ventures and its 45-storey tower at 1133 Avenue of the Americas ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/08/28/skylines-robotic-window-washer-debuts-at-durst-office-tower)) [Fact]. Related, Brookfield, Tishman Speyer, Vornado and RXR are plausible targets, but the notes found no documented pilots [Inference]. Public transport agencies (MTA, PANYNJ, NYC DOT) buy through TTL. Health systems such as Mount Sinai and Northwell buy AI software ([Becker's](https://www.beckershospitalreview.com/healthcare-information-technology/ai/mount-sinai-inks-3-year-ai-partnership-with-nvidia); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20211028005371/en/Northwell-Health-and-Aegis-Ventures-to-form-first-of-its-kind-artificial-intelligence-venture-to-drive-better-more-equitable-and-lower-cost-healthcare)), but the notes found no NYC hospital robotics pilot. The reference point is upstate: Rochester Regional runs eight Moxi robots on a usage-tied subscription ([Rochester Regional](https://www.rochesterregional.org/hub/moxi-robot-rgh-unity-hospital)) [Fact]. For this report's recommended logistics wedge, the buyers are **NJ 3PLs and distributors**, a gap the notes did not cover.

**Programmes and grants.** Transit Tech Lab (annual, eight-week PoCs; [Transit Innovation Partnership](https://transitinnovation.org/news/transit-tech-lab-selects-eighteen-companies-to-collaborate-with-new-york-transportation-agencies-on-transit-innovation)). NYCEDC Pilots at BAT gives robotics firms a Brooklyn test site for customer demos ([Brooklyn Reporter](https://brooklynreporter.com/2025/04/three-new-companies-join-pilots-at-bat-program)). The NYC DOT × Newlab Innovation Studio ran pilots from October 2025 to January 2026 ([press release](https://www.finanzen.net/nachricht/aktien/newlab-and-nyc-dot-launch-open-call-for-ai-startups-to-pilot-street-level-transportation-safety-and-infrastructure-solutions-14651491)). Blue Highways and the microhub programme create waterfront and last-mile freight openings, with the first micro-freight landing slated for Q4 2027 ([NYCEDC](https://edc.nyc/node/7317)). Empire AI is a state-backed compute consortium of about US$400M ([Governor's office](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-launches-empire-ai-consortium-make-new-york-global-leader-artificial)). NSF SBIR is the federal grant route [Fact].

**Events and partners.** New York Robotics is a nonprofit covering about 160 regional startups, with partners including J.P. Morgan, AlleyCorp, Newlab and Standard Bots. AlleyCorp runs Deep Tech NY, and Newlab has more than 300 members ([The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/new-york-robotics-launches-160-startups-ecosystem/)) [Fact]. ERA, Techstars NYC, Cornell Tech and NYU Tandon were **not verified**.

**Regulatory steps.** Keep the first deployment **indoors and on private property**. NYC DOT sent FedEx a cease-and-desist over its sidewalk robot in 2019 ([TechSpot](https://www.techspot.com/news/82943-new-york-tells-fedex-get-robot-off-city.html)) [Fact]. If the AI touches hiring or worker evaluation, Local Law 144 bias audits apply ([NY OSC audit](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools)). The RAISE Act targets frontier-model developers, not this company ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/governor-hochul-signs-new-yorks-ai-safety-act-220503930.html)) [Fact]. Start SOC 2. Type I takes about 8–12 weeks ([Promise Legal](https://promise.legal/guides/soc2-roadmap)) [Fact]. A vendor claim that SOC 2 Type II cuts the enterprise cycle from **8.4 to 5.0 months** is unverified ([Wolfia](https://cdn.wolfia.com/blog/soc-2-compliance-requirements-complete-guide)). For public-facing robots, consider UL 3300.

**Labour and politics.** 32BJ SEIU publicly criticised Skyline's robot ([The Real Deal](https://therealdeal.com/new-york/2024/08/28/skylines-robotic-window-washer-debuts-at-durst-office-tower)). The NYPD's Digidog and K5 drew sustained advocacy opposition ([S.T.O.P.](https://www.stopspying.org/latest-news/2024/2/2/stop-welcomes-end-of-nypd-times-sq-robot-pilot)) [Fact]. Position the robot around hazardous or unfilled tasks, keep a human in the loop, route deployment through the unionised incumbent contractor, and **avoid surveillance framing** [Inference].

### Paris / France: build a three-part non-dilutive stack and price under the thresholds

**Top buyers.** These are CAC 40 and SBF 120 operators with labour or ergonomic pain: Renault (Wandercraft), Cdiscount and Decathlon (Exotec; [Le Monde Informatique](https://www.lemondeinformatique.fr/actualites/lire-exotec-solutions-deploie-ses-robots-logistiques-chez-les-e-commercants-73922.html)), and the JCLFT signatory list. That list includes **730+ signatory companies** as of June 2025 ([Siècle Digital](https://siecledigital.fr/2025/06/16/je-choisis-la-french-tech-le-programme-franchit-le-cap-du-milliard-deuros-et-vise-leurope/)) and retailers such as Fnac Darty ([Fnac Darty](https://www.fnacdarty.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/cp-170626-cpvivatech-jclft-1.pdf)) [Fact]. Public operators such as SNCF and RATP depots, AP-HP hospital logistics, Groupe ADP and La Poste are plausible achat-innovant buyers. Some are "entités adjudicatrices" under different rules, so check each [Inference]. The binding constraint is conversion. EY's barometer reports that large French groups name **moving from POC to deployment and meeting cybersecurity requirements** as their two main brakes on buying from startups ([EY, summary only](https://www.ey.com/fr_fr/insights/fast-growing-companies/performance-de-l-ecosysteme-d-innovation-francais)) [Fact]. Sopra Steria finds **48%** of French companies struggle to find the right startups ([Solutions Numériques](https://www.solutions-numeriques.com/?p=237761)) [Fact]. That second finding is an opening for well-targeted outreach.

**Programmes and grants.** Bourse French Tech offers up to **€30K** ([Legalstart](https://www.legalstart.fr/fiches-pratiques/aides-creation-entreprise/bourse-french-tech/)) and BFT Émergence up to **€90K** ([Copeps](https://copeps.fr/actualites/espace-pme-innovation/)). i-Lab is €500–600K (sources conflict). i-Nov ranges from €200K to €2.25M. France 2030's robotics strategy has mobilised **€250M+** across 100+ projects, including a June 2026 round of **31 laureates for €68M** ([entreprises.gouv.fr](https://www.entreprises.gouv.fr/espace-presse/france-2030-31-nouveaux-projets-pour-accelerer-lemergence-dune-filiere-francaise)). The CII is now **20%**, capped at €80K a year ([Deloitte Avocats](https://blog.avocats.deloitte.fr/lf-et-lfss-pour-2025-panorama-des-differentes-mesures-prises-en-matiere-de-fiscalite-de-la-rd/)). CIR and JEI status now require R&D of at least **20%** of charges ([Legifiscal](https://www.legifiscal.fr/actualites-fiscales/4186-jei-rehaussement-20-seuil-depenses-recherche.html)). The EIC Accelerator's 2026 cut-offs ended on 4 November 2026, and **2027 dates are not yet confirmed** [Fact].

**Events and partners.** VivaTech in June is where JCLFT and France 2030 announcements land ([FrenchWeb](https://www.frenchweb.fr/vivatech-2025-letat-muscle-sa-strategie-industrielle-pour-les-startups-francaises-avec-un-milliard-deuros-fleches/455396)). Regional clusters such as Minalogic and Atlanpole relay calls for projects [Fact]. Global Industrie is the more relevant show for plant buyers [Inference]. Station F, Hub France IA, Paris&Co, Le Village by CA, Wilco, Agoranov, Systematic, Cap Digital, Inria and CEA-List are well-known names but **were not verified**.

**Regulatory steps.** The EU Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 **applies from 20 January 2027** ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/1230/oj)). It adds requirements for AI and self-evolving safety functions, so CE marking work must target it now. AI Act high-risk obligations moved to **2 December 2027** for standalone systems and **2 August 2028** for AI embedded in regulated products, after the provisional Digital Omnibus deal of 7 May 2026 ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/The-Digital-AI-Omnibus-Proposed-deferral-of-high-risk-AI-obligations-under-the-AI-Act)). The revised Product Liability Directive covers software and must be transposed by 9 December 2026 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj)). The Cyber Resilience Act's reporting duties started on 11 September 2026 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj)) [Fact; dates from official texts not re-fetched]. Plan for GDPR/CNIL work (a DPIA if cameras capture workers) and **CSE works-council consultation**, which adds weeks to any deployment that changes working conditions [Inference].

**Payment.** The LME cap is 60 days from invoice or 45 days end-of-month ([Légifrance L441-10](https://www.legifrance.gouv.fr/codes/article_lc/LEGIARTI000038414279)). Late payment still traps about **€13bn** of SME cash ([TPE Actu](https://tpeactu.fr/2026/09/26/retards-paiement-13-milliards-tpe-pme-grandes-entreprises/)) [Fact]. Prefer Pacte PME signatories, which are mostly paying on time ([Pacte PME](https://pactepme.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/CP-PactePME-Les-grands-comptes-francais-amplifient-leurs-efforts-pour-soutenir-la-resilience-des-PME-grace-a-des-pratiques-de-paiement-responsables-22-janvier-2025.pdf)), and public buyers, who must pay within 30 days [Fact]. Ask for a 30–40% upfront hardware milestone and invoice against a signed PV de réception [Inference].

## 6. Seven pitfalls sink AI-robotics pilots, and each has a known countermeasure

**Pilot purgatory.** The headline numbers come from enterprise generative AI, not robotics. MIT NANDA found about **95%** of genAI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/08/18/mit-report-95-percent-generative-ai-pilots-at-companies-failing-cfo/)), and Gartner predicted **at least 30%** of genAI projects would be abandoned after POC ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2024-07-29-gartner-predicts-30-percent-of-generative-ai-projects-will-be-abandoned-after-proof-of-concept-by-end-of-2025)) [Fact]. For robotics, Innovation Endeavors says "very few pilots have converted to scaled contracts" ([Innovation Endeavors](https://innovationendeavors.com/insights/lessons-learned-the-hard-way-robotics)) [Opinion]. MIT's finding that purchased or partnered solutions succeed about **67%** of the time, against roughly one-third for internal builds, is a useful sales argument ([Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/mit-enterprise-ai-pilots-fail-revenues)) [Fact, secondary]. **Avoid it** by charging for the pilot, getting written KPIs and a decision date signed by the economic buyer, pre-agreeing production price and volume, deploying in production rather than a lab cell, and holding an executive readout at week 8–12 [Inference]. In France, insist on a business-unit budget owner, because EY names POC-to-deployment as the main brake.

**Trust and safety certification.** The applicable standards are ISO 10218 (industrial arms; revised 2025), ISO 3691-4 (AMRs and driverless trucks), ISO 13482 (personal-care robots), UL 3300 (service and public-facing robots, now OSHA-recognised; [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260303951641/en/UL-Solutions-Grants-Its-First-Certification-for-a-Public-Facing-Robot-to-Simbe%E2%80%99s-Tally-Autonomous-Mobile-Robot/)), and the EU Machinery Regulation from January 2027. The notes flag that the standards map comes from general knowledge and **certification costs and timelines were not sourced** [Inference]. Buyers judge uptime and interventions more than model accuracy. **Avoid it** by designing to the standard from day one, using human-in-the-loop or teleoperation fallback (as Ozmo and Calvin did), and putting an insurance certificate and safety file in the first-meeting pack [Inference].

**Privacy law by region.** NYC: Local Law 144 if the AI evaluates workers. Biometric signage rules for public-facing cameras are unverified. EU/France: GDPR, a CNIL-style DPIA, and CSE consultation for workplace monitoring. UAE: PDPL, which has an uncertain implementation status, plus DIFC Regulation 10 for autonomous systems. India: DPDP obligations from May 2027. **Avoid it** with on-device processing, face blurring, short retention, regional hosting (EU and UAE cloud regions), and a ready data processing agreement template [Inference].

**Hardware logistics and service radius.** US tariffs drove the 2025 mobile-robot forecast cut. China installs **54%** of the world's industrial robots ([iConnect007](https://iconnect007.com/article/147063/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years/147060/smt)) [Fact], which gives Chinese hardware a cost advantage. India's import tax load is about 28% before credits, and the UAE needs a separate entity for import and storage. **Avoid it** by keeping the first customers within a 2–4 hour drive of the service team, holding a spare unit per site, and assembling locally where "Make in India" preference helps [Inference].

**ROI that doesn't close.** Buyers' payback expectations range from 1–3 years ([AutoStore](https://www.autostoresystem.com/insights/the-automation-investment-landscape)) to "five years or less" ([TT News](https://ttnews.com/articles/warehouse-automation)) [Opinion/Vendor]. In Maharashtra, unskilled wages of about **₹13,600–14,960 a month** ([Keka](https://www.keka.com/library/minimum-wages/maharashtra)) [Fact] mean a ₹25–35 lakh robot replacing one shift pays back in **more than 10 years**, and in about 4–5 years on three shifts [Inference]. **Avoid it** by selling multi-shift operation, safety, throughput, space density and labour availability, and by pricing RaaS below the loaded labour cost it replaces [Inference].

**Incumbents and commoditising "robot brains".** The incumbents are ABB, FANUC, KUKA and Locus, Micropolis in Dubai, and Addverb and GreyOrange in India. Foundation-model providers are commoditising the AI layer. **Avoid it** by competing on vertical workflow depth, deployment speed and service, not on model IP, and by partnering with integrators rather than fighting them [Inference].

**Labour and political backlash.** This is most acute in NYC (32BJ, NYPD robot controversies) and real in French workplaces (CSE). UNITE HERE contracts elsewhere require **30–165 days'** notice before technology changes ([Hotel Dive](https://www.hoteldive.com/news/hospitality-union-tech-battles/734541/)) [Fact]. **Avoid it** by framing the robot around unfilled or hazardous tasks and engaging unions and works councils before deployment [Inference].

## 7. Price the first pilot at two to three months of RaaS plus integration, credited on conversion

The notes converge on a structure, though the exact figures are an **[Inference]**: a **paid pilot of 8–12 weeks with 1–3 robots on site**, priced at roughly two to three months of RaaS per unit plus a one-time integration fee, and **100% credited** toward a 12–36 month RaaS contract if named KPIs are met. Example KPIs are at least X picks per hour, at least 99.x% accuracy and at least 95% uptime. The conversion clause names the economic buyer as signatory and commits N units at a pre-agreed price within 30 days. A non-binding LOI with indicative volume ("up to 20 units at X a month") aligns the customer internally before the pilot starts. Enterprises that want the asset on their balance sheet can be offered capex plus a software and maintenance subscription. Outcome or per-pick pricing (US$0.03–0.06 per pick is quoted for AMRs; [Robotomated](https://robotomated.com/learn/market/robotics-as-a-service-raas-guide)) [Vendor] fits only when the vendor controls enough of the workflow to guarantee throughput. Warn buyers, and yourself, that consumption pricing that looks cheap in a pilot "compounds" in production ([Monetizely](https://www.getmonetizely.com/articles/understanding-agentic-ai-pricing-models-for-enterprise-software-a-complete-guide)) [Opinion].

## 10. Experts disagree on free pilots, RaaS, channels and the "95% fail" statistic

**Paid versus free pilots.** Fero Labs' rule was "don't do free work," even at the cost of the customer ([Frontlines](https://www.frontlines.io/?p=5723)) [Opinion], and Sierra charged all its design partners. Other guides allow usage-limited free trials ([CallSphere](https://callsphere.ai/blog/vw7c-free-pilot-vs-paid-poc-ai-2026)) [Vendor]. Some founders argue that "by the time a major customer agrees to a pilot, the product question is already settled," so the pilot is about internal politics ([Frontlines](https://www.frontlines.io/climate-tech-enterprise-sales/)) [Opinion]. That view implies a discounted pilot is acceptable for a marquee logo if the conversion terms are strict. **My position [Inference]:** charge for robotics pilots. Hardware and integration costs make unpaid pilots the most common route into purgatory. Make an exception only for a flagship government or Fortune 500 site with written conversion terms.

**RaaS versus capex.** RaaS lowers adoption barriers ([Built In](https://builtin.com/robotics/robotics-as-a-service-raas)). Critics say vendor balance sheets "don't scale" and robotics burn is 3–5x SaaS burn ([peaq](https://www.peaq.xyz/blog/why-robots-dont-deploy-at-scale-unusual-assets-need-unusual-financing)) [Opinion, vendor]. RaaS margins also sit below both hardware and software margins ([IdeaProof](https://ideaproof.io/failure/locus-robotics)) [Opinion, unsourced post-mortem]. **Resolution [Inference]:** sell RaaS and finance it off your balance sheet.

**Direct versus channel.** Investors say deploy the first units directly ([Robotics 24/7](https://www.robotics247.com/article/robotics_invest_panelists_discuss_startup_strategy_vc_criteria_in_boston/autonomy)). Industry analysts say selling "direct, alone" is fading in favour of ABB and FANUC-style partner ecosystems ([channel-mix analysis](https://cube-clownfish-lp7x.squarespace.com/artificial-intelligence-robotics/build-buy-or-partner-the-new-robotics-channel-mix)) [Opinion]. The NYC evidence partly supports the analysts: Skyline's first customer came through an incumbent contractor.

**How bad pilot failure really is.** MIT's 95% figure rests on 150 interviews and a narrow "P&L impact within six months" definition, and critics have challenged the sample ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/mit-report-95-generative-ai-105412686.html)). Gartner's 30% abandonment forecast is far lower. Menlo reports enterprise genAI spend tripled to **US$37B** as buyers moved to production ([PE Hub](https://www.pehub.com/ai-boom-is-being-driven-by-rapid-enterprise-adoption/)) [Fact]. None of these measures robotics.

**Other live disputes.** Payback hurdles range from 1–3 years to five. Cold-email benchmarks vary by 7x because of denominators. AI pricing data conflicts: 23% outcome-based against 58% seat-based in different ICONIQ-derived summaries ([Withorb](https://www.withorb.com/blog/outcome-based-pricing-statistics)). The warehouse market was forecast down, then up, within six months. In France, one critic warns the French Tech "doit faire attention à ce qu'elle choisit" ([Next.ink](https://next.ink/223104/je-choisis-la-french-tech-mais-la-french-tech-doit-faire-attention-a-ce-quelle-choisit/)) [Opinion], and the notes found **no independent evaluation** of JCLFT's revenue impact. The vertical-versus-horizontal debate was **not sourced** from VCs. Every sourced robotics first customer, however, was a narrow vertical wedge, including the humanoid companies [Inference].

### Gaps a reader should weigh

The notes could not reach First Round Review, IFR, Menlo, arnon.dk, EY, La French Tech or Gibson Dunn directly, so several figures rest on search summaries. The notes found **no** robotics pilot-to-paid conversion rates, **no** disclosed values for the Figure, Agility or Dexterity anchor deals, **no** EUR or AED pilot price benchmarks, **no** certification cost or timeline data, **no** US-specific payment-term data, **no** BIS status for robots in India, and **no** verified details on many named accelerators (ERA, Station F programmes, NASSCOM, TiE, Mahindra, Godrej and L&T programmes). The NJ logistics market, which this report recommends as NYC's entry point, was **not researched**. The 2027 EIC cut-offs and France's possible €140K innovative-purchase threshold are unconfirmed.

## Conclusion

The strongest finding is that **the first customer in AI-robotics is bought with risk transfer, not won with technology**. Every documented anchor deal removed a specific risk for the buyer. Formic's RaaS removed capex risk, Skyline's incumbent contractor removed labour risk, Wandercraft's supervised single task removed safety risk, and BMW's milestone gates removed performance risk. A startup's pilot design is therefore a product in its own right. The four cities differ mainly in **which risk-transfer instrument the state provides**: France provides grants and no-tender purchasing, Dubai a top-down mandate and accelerator access, India pilot work orders and payment-term leverage, and New York agency PoCs but little else. That is why the cheapest place to win customer one (Paris) is not the place where customer ten pays most (New York).

The second insight is about timing. The window between now and early 2027 is unusually loaded. The EU Machinery Regulation takes effect on **20 January 2027**, AI Act high-risk duties follow in 2027–2028, India's DPDP duties start in May 2027, and GITEX falls in December 2026. A company that builds its safety file, data-protection pack and paid-pilot template **once**, to the strictest regime (the EU), can reuse them in all four cities. Compliance becomes a sales asset rather than a tax. The main uncertainty is conversion: until the first paid pilot hits its KPIs and triggers a pre-priced contract, every number in this report is a plan, not a benchmark.
