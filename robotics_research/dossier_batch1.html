1 · Executive summary

  1. Money is flooding in. Crunchbase counts ~$9.9B of robotics venture funding in Q3 2026, against $4.3B in Q3 2025, and roughly $10B per quarter through 2026 [86][85]. Physical AI as a broader category raised $47.4B across 521 deals in H1 2026 [87]. PitchBook counted $27.6B for all of 2025 [88].
  2. Mega-seeds are now normal. Walden Robotics raised a $300M seed at a $1.1B valuation (Jul 2026). Robotics VCs are raising billion-dollar funds: Eclipse $1.3B, Lux $1.5B, and Khosla reportedly targeting $5.5B.
  3. Hardware needs bigger, longer capital. Eclipse built a growth fund specifically because hardware companies outgrow what software-style VCs will fund. Root keeps about two-thirds of each fund for follow-ons and bridges.
  4. Investors disagree on where the technology is. Bessemer says “GPT-2.5 moment”, with real capability but a wide lab-to-field gap [63]. Khosla's Ethan Choi expects a “GPT-3 moment” in 2026. Vinod Khosla still expects most robotics valuations to fall by 2030.
  5. Deployment is the new moat. Bay Area event titles in 2026 include “Deployment is the Moat” and “Selling Outcomes, Not Robots”. The money is shifting towards proof of real-world uptime and robots-as-a-service pricing.
  6. Buyers are sceptical. Flexport's CEO runs about 5M sq ft of fulfilment space, doubts humanoids fit that work, and will buy robots but not build them [18]. ROI-driven buyers, not demos, decide revenue.
  7. Corporates want to be the parts supplier. Bosch (Neura, Humanoid), NVIDIA (chips plus NVentures) and Intel (RealSense, Figure) invest to own the component layer of humanoids.
  8. Defence is the fastest commercial path. Bessemer expects the biggest robotics outcomes to come from defence, and drones and defence are a whole category on the map.
  9. The supply chain is the bottleneck and India's opening. Indian robot makers import actuators and sensors from Germany, Japan, Korea and China [89]. The US is pushing to source away from China because of tariffs and China+1, and Fictiv/MISUMI is publicly reorganising around a “global sourcing shift”.
  10. Lawyers and contract manufacturers are under-used information sources. Gunderson sees dozens of robotics term sheets, and Sanmina and Fictiv see real build volumes. Calls to them teach economics faster than calls to robot companies.

4 · Recurring problems (ranked by frequency in sources)

  1. Lab-to-field gap: demos work, but reliability in messy real environments does not (Bessemer, deployment-themed events).
  2. Capital intensity: hardware needs far more money and time, hence growth funds, large reserves and mega-rounds.
  3. Proving ROI to buyers: buyers like Flexport and Prologis want payback, not demos.
  4. Component supply and tariffs: dependence on China and imports for actuators, motors and sensors; US tariff volatility (Flexport, Fictiv).
  5. Safety and regulation: safety cases, incident analysis and export controls for drones and defence (Exponent, Orrick).
  6. Valuation risk: Vinod Khosla expects most robotics valuations to fall by 2030.

7 · Call plan, time zones & outreach

When to call

Mumbai is IST (UTC+5:30). The Bay Area is on PST (UTC−8) from 1 Nov 2026 to 14 Mar 2027, which puts Mumbai 13.5 hours ahead. 9:00–11:30 am Pacific = 10:30 pm–1:00 am IST. When PDT returns (UTC−7), the same window is 9:30 pm–midnight IST. Avoid Pacific Fridays and US holidays (Thanksgiving is 26 Nov 2026).

Sequence

  1. Warm intro: ask Embodied Collective, SVRC or office-hour hosts first.
  2. LinkedIn connection note.
  3. Short email (use the firm's contact page or the published lawyer or partner email).
  4. Propose two specific 20-minute slots in their time zone.
  5. One follow-up after 5–7 days, then stop.

Main office numbers are for reaching a receptionist or front desk only (for example, to ask for the right contact person or the best email). Do not cold-call individuals.
