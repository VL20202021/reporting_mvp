Building on NVIDIA: billion-dollar business opportunities

Industry verticals, all capital tiers, and a comparison of locations worldwide  ·  Research report as of 18 November 2026

Bottom line

  • Can a billion-dollar business be built on NVIDIA products? Yes, and many already have been. NVIDIA reported $115.8B in revenue for one quarter (Q3 FY2027, up 103% from a year earlier), and $107.6B of that was Data Center. Companies that build on its stack are worth billions: Harvey (legal AI) ~$15.5B, Chai Discovery (AI drug design) $3.8B, Crusoe (GPU cloud) ~$30.9B, Skild AI (robotics) ~$14B.
  • Using NVIDIA products is not a moat by itself. Every competitor can buy the same GPUs and software. The value goes to companies that own one of four scarce things: proprietary data, a regulated workflow (clinical, insurance, legal or financial), a distribution or partnership channel (governments, telecoms, manufacturers), or cheap, available power.
  • The best odds are in vertical AI. Software that automates one high-value, regulated workflow can reach a $1B valuation on roughly $50–100M in annual recurring revenue. A GPU cloud usually needs billions in capex to get there.
  • Where to build depends on the model. Vertical AI software goes to the US, UK or EU hubs nearest its buyers and regulators. GPU infrastructure goes where power is cheap and available and state partners exist: Texas, the Nordics, Saudi Arabia/UAE, and India (subsidised). Physical AI and robotics go to manufacturing economies: Germany, South Korea, Japan and the US.
Two recent events you should account for (both within the last week):
  1. NVIDIA leadership change. Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on 12 Nov 2026. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, and the board said it will not reinstate him. CNN reported about $1.76T in lost market value. The roadmap and partnerships continue, but Gulf and Asian partners now expect slower timelines and more compliance scrutiny.
  2. New US export rule (16 Nov 2026). It replaces the old country-tier "AI Diffusion" system. Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil now need licences for advanced chips. Any data centre that gives China-parented companies more than 12M TPP of compute needs a licence; existing operators have 90 days to get one. This changes where GPU infrastructure can be built safely.
Design any business to survive dependence on a single vendor: build software that can also run on other accelerators where practical, and do not rely on one sovereign deal.

2. Vertical opportunities

Each vertical below lists what to build, which NVIDIA components it uses, evidence that it can reach $1B, and where to build it. They are ordered roughly by how likely they are to reach $1B for a new entrant.

#1 Healthcare: clinical workflow AI (ambient documentation, coding, radiology workflow)

What to buildAI that drafts clinical notes, codes encounters for billing, triages imaging worklists, or automates prior authorisations. Narrow wedges are still open: specialty-specific tools (oncology, behavioural health, dental, veterinary), non-English markets, and revenue-cycle agents. NVIDIA stackNIM and NeMo for hosting and fine-tuning language and speech models, Riva for speech, Clara and MONAI for imaging, Holoscan for real-time devices. Proof of $1BAbridge reached a $5.3B valuation (2025, confirmed). It is deployed across Kaiser Permanente's 40 hospitals and 600+ offices and signed an NVIDIA partnership in June 2026. Ambient documentation was the most heavily funded healthcare-AI category in 2025, with an estimated ~$600M of annual spend. CapitalSeed $2–5M; reaching $1B usually takes $100M+ raised. A bootstrapped start is possible as a specialty-clinic product. WhereUS (biggest market for paying customers, with FDA and payer complexity that protects incumbents), UK (NHS scale), Germany and France (strict data residency favours local vendors), India (low-cost build, plus a domestic market).

#2 Life sciences: AI drug and protein design

What to buildA generative biology platform (antibodies, enzymes, small molecules) that earns money from pharma partnerships and milestones, or a narrower tool such as ADMET prediction, lab automation, or CRO-as-a-service on BioNeMo. NVIDIA stackBioNeMo (protein and molecule models), DGX Cloud, Clara Parabricks (genomics). Proof of $1BChai Discovery raised a $400M Series C at $3.8B (Jul 2026). Isomorphic Labs raised a $2.1B Series B. Xaira raised a $1.3B debut round. Warp Sciences was valued at $865M before releasing a product. CapitalVC tier and up. You need $20–100M+ and wet-lab capacity, and investors expect elite founding teams. It is binary: very high payoffs, long timelines. WhereBoston/Cambridge and the SF Bay Area (pharma partners and talent), London and Cambridge UK (Isomorphic and DeepMind ecosystem), Switzerland (Basel pharma cluster).

#3 Climate, weather and catastrophe-risk intelligence

What to buildHyper-local forecasts and risk scores sold to insurers and reinsurers, renewable-energy operators and traders, agriculture, logistics, and governments. NVIDIA stackEarth-2 open weather models, launched Jan 2026 and described as up to 1,000× faster than physics simulation. Also Omniverse for visualisation. Proof of demandBrightband runs Earth-2 operationally. Vaisala Xweather sells it to energy, maritime and insurance customers. AXA and JBA use it for extreme-event risk, and insurers run ensembles of about 10,000 members. TotalEnergies, Eni and GCL are testing it for solar and grid forecasting. Path to $1BBecome the system of record for climate risk in one large market, such as property insurance pricing or power trading. Because the models are open, the moat is your proprietary observations, calibration and integration with customers' pricing systems. CapitalSeed $1–5M. Low compute cost compared with drug discovery. WhereZurich and London (reinsurance hubs), the Nordics and Germany (energy trading), the US (property catastrophe market), India and Southeast Asia (monsoon and agriculture risk).

#4 Industrial AI: digital twins, vision QA and robotics integration

What to buildFactory and warehouse digital twins, vision-based quality inspection, robot simulation-to-deployment services, and fleet software for humanoids or AMRs. Integrators that turn NVIDIA's platform into results at a plant are scarce. NVIDIA stackOmniverse, Isaac (sim and robot learning), Cosmos (world models), Metropolis (vision AI), Jetson (edge). Proof of $1BFigure AI ~$39B, Skild AI ~$14B (NVIDIA's NVentures invested), Physical Intelligence $5.6B confirmed. Robot makers need huge capital; software and integration layers need far less. CapitalBootstrapped (integration consultancy) up to $100M+ (robot hardware). WhereGermany (automotive and machinery), South Korea (Samsung, SK and Hyundai each plan 50k+ GPU AI factories as part of a 260k-GPU national plan), Japan (labour shortages), and the US Midwest/South plus Mexico (reshoring).

#5 Financial services: fraud, risk and compliance AI

What to buildReal-time fraud and AML detection, credit underwriting agents, trade surveillance, and agents that automate regulatory reporting for banks, payment firms and insurers. NVIDIA stackRAPIDS and cuDF (GPU data processing), Morpheus (security), NIM and NeMo agents, graph neural networks. EvidenceJensen Huang has said "classical" accelerated computing, including fraud detection and recommendation systems, is about a third of NVIDIA's business and is growing 30–40% a year. I found no reliable independent size for this market. CapitalSeed $2–10M. Sales cycles are long, but contracts are large and sticky. WhereNew York, London, Singapore (finance hub; the new chip-licence rule affects data centres, not software companies), Dubai/Abu Dhabi (DIFC/ADGM), and Mumbai/Bengaluru (UPI-scale payments fraud).

#6 Telecom: AI-RAN and edge inference

What to buildAI-RAN applications (network optimisation, energy saving), monetisation of telecom edge inference, and software that sells spare RAN GPU capacity as an edge cloud. NVIDIA stackAerial and the ARC (Aerial RAN Computer), with Nokia anyRAN. EvidenceT-Mobile completed the first AI-RAN call on a live commercial network. Nearly 20 operators announced collaborations (Oct 2026), and commercial services are targeted for 2027. It is early, which is good for timing but means few paying customers yet. CapitalVC tier. You need telecom-grade engineering and long operator sales cycles. WhereFinland and Sweden (Nokia and Ericsson ecosystems), the US (T-Mobile), South Korea and Japan (early 5G/6G adopters).

Also strong: other regulated professional workflows (legal, insurance claims, accounting)

These are not tied to a specific NVIDIA framework, but they run on NIM and NeMo and are the clearest evidence for vertical AI: Harvey ~$15.5B (Sep 2026), Legora $8.8B (Oct 2026), EvenUp $2B. About $3B went into pure-play vertical AI across 82 deals in H1 2026. Room remains in non-English jurisdictions (DACH, France, Japan, Brazil, the Gulf in Arabic) and in sub-verticals (immigration, tax, insurance claims).

5. Key risks

  • Vendor and governance risk at NVIDIA. A CEO transition after a misconduct disclosure, plus congressional inquiries. Its products remain the standard, but plan for slower partnership decisions and design for portability across accelerators.
  • Export-control risk. Rules changed again on 16 Nov 2026. Infrastructure that serves Chinese-parented customers, or sits in the five listed countries, needs licences.
  • Supply constraints. NVIDIA's own guidance is supply-constrained, not demand-constrained. Small buyers wait longest for new GPUs, so rent capacity early.
  • Volatile GPU prices. H100 rental fell about 60% from 2023 to early 2026, then rose again in 2026; for example, Nebius raised its on-demand H100 price from $3.85 to $4.50/hr in Oct 2026. Price your product on value delivered, not on compute cost plus a margin.
  • Pressure from hyperscalers and foundation-model companies. Horizontal features get absorbed by the big platforms. Stay vertical and own the workflow and the data.
  • Regulation. FDA (devices and clinical decision support), the EU AI Act (high-risk systems), and financial regulators. This costs money, but it is also the moat once you have cleared it.

6. Recommended next steps

  1. Pick one vertical where you have an unfair advantage: domain expertise, customer access, or proprietary data. The ranking above is a starting point, but founder–market fit matters more.
  2. Choose the capital tier honestly. Without $100M+ and access to power, skip infrastructure and build software, renting compute from neoclouds.
  3. Join NVIDIA Inception (free, no equity) for cheaper AI Enterprise and NIM licensing and go-to-market co-marketing.
  4. Validate with 10–20 buyer interviews in the chosen vertical and region before building, and aim for a paid pilot within 90 days.
  5. Set your location by buyer and regulator, not by power (for software). For infrastructure, choose locations outside the new licence-list countries.
